## Three parts make a receptionist

Every AI Employee, including the AI Receptionist, is built from the same three parts. Configuring yours means deciding who it is, what it can do, and what it knows. Flip each one:

Your receptionist serves your business, so it lives in your workspace: **Partner Center**. Everything in this step happens there.

## Meet your workforce

Open `AI` → `Workforce`. The page that loads is your **AI Workforce**, and every AI Employee you have sits on it as a card.

You already have more than one. The **Chat Receptionist** answers anyone messaging your business. **Ash** is the voice receptionist and answers the phone. The **AI Sales Assistant** works on your recorded sales calls. Alongside them sit any custom employees, and a `Create` button in the top right for building your own.

Two things on every card are worth reading before you click anything:

- **The buttons differ by employee.** The Chat Receptionist offers `Chat` and `Configure`. Ash offers `Call`, `Try it`, and `Configure`, because you test a voice receptionist by phoning it.
- **Autonomy** is shown on the two receptionist cards as a level, and it is a label rather than a control. The info icon beside it explains the four levels; there is nothing to set. Custom employees show no autonomy at all.

:::info Which plan carries which receptionist
The **Starter** plan includes the AI Chat Receptionist, which is the one this step configures. The **Professional** plan and above add the AI Voice Receptionist and the social channels, Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Two gates run in parallel and it is worth keeping them apart: your own subscription decides what you get, and a client gets whichever edition of Conversations AI is active on their account. [This guide breaks down what each plan includes](/ai/ai-workforce/ai-receptionist-features-by-plan).
:::

Go to `AI` → `Workforce`.

## Name it, and read what it already does

The Configure page is one screen with everything on it. On the left: the photo, the **Name** field, the **Autonomy** level, and a `View prompt` button. On the right, in order: **Triggers**, **Profile**, **Capabilities**, **Knowledge sources**, and **Automations**.

Start with the name, because the AI knows its own name and introduces itself with it. Then read before you write, because two things are already done for you.

Under **Capabilities** you will find **Capture leads**: "Collects contact information, creates a new contact, and lets the contact know someone on your team will follow up." That is the capability that turns a chat into a record in your CRM, and it is on before you touch anything.

<LabCallout title="make it yours, then read the role">
Go to the receptionist's `Configure` page.

<LabChecklist
  storageKey="turn-on-receptionist-lab-2"
  steps={[
    <>In <strong>Name</strong>, replace "Chat Receptionist" with a name that fits your brand, and set a photo.</>,
    <>Under <strong>Profile</strong>, expand <code>Role</code>. This is the job description and behavioural guidelines it already runs on. Read it before changing a word.</>,
    <>Select <code>View prompt</code> to see the full instruction the AI actually receives.</>,
    <>Under <strong>Capabilities</strong>, find <strong>Capture leads</strong> and read its description. Its <code>⋮</code> menu is where you edit or remove it, and <code>Learn more</code> opens its documentation.</>,
    <>Select <code>Save</code>.</>,
  ]}
  confirm={<>The receptionist carries your name and photo, and you have read both the role it runs on and the Capture leads capability it already has.</>}
/>
</LabCallout>

Reading first is the habit worth forming. The role that ships is a working one, and most first-day changes are small additions to it rather than rewrites.

## Give it knowledge

**Knowledge sources** on the same page is where you choose what the AI can pull from when it answers. Your website homepage is already one of them, included by default, which is why the receptionist can say something useful before you configure anything at all. Every source lists its address, when it last refreshed, and how many pages it trained on, so you can always see exactly what the AI is working from.

Adding one takes two clicks rather than one. The `Add knowledge` field first offers knowledge you already have; `+ Add new knowledge` at the top of that list is what opens the choice of three:

- **Text** for content related to your business, such as FAQs or custom information
- **Website** to retrieve content from a website
- **File Upload** for a document or file

<LabCallout title="add the rest of your website">
Go to the same `Configure` page, under **Knowledge sources**.

<LabChecklist
  storageKey="turn-on-receptionist-lab-3"
  steps={[
    <>Read the sources already listed, and note the page count on each one.</>,
    <>Select the <code>Add knowledge</code> field at the bottom of the list. It opens a dropdown of knowledge you already have.</>,
    <>Select <code>+ Add new knowledge</code> at the top of that dropdown, then choose <strong>Website</strong>.</>,
    <>Enter your website address. It has to be reachable without a login, so check it in an incognito window if you are unsure.</>,
    <>Save, then give the source time to finish training.</>,
    <>Come back and read its line: the date it last refreshed, and how many pages it trained on out of how many it found.</>,
  ]}
  confirm={<>Your site appears as a source with a page count, and you know exactly how much of it the AI learned.</>}
/>
</LabCallout>

Anything that lives nowhere on your site (your hours, your prices, what makes you different) goes in through **Text** as a written entry, and a price list or a policy document goes in through **File Upload**. For every source type, crawl mode, and refresh option, [the Knowledge Base guide](/ai/knowledge-base/) covers it, and `AI` → `Knowledge Base` is where you manage sources across every employee at once.

</LessonSection>

## Put it to practice

`Chat` on the receptionist's card opens a conversation between you and your employee. It is private to you and it keeps a history, and it is where you try your receptionist out: you chat with it, you audit the answers, and you fix what is off. There is no separate test page to find.

<LabCallout title="interview your receptionist">
Go to `AI` → `Workforce`, then `Chat` on the Chat Receptionist card.

<LabChecklist
  storageKey="turn-on-receptionist-lab-4"
  steps={[
    <>Ask the three questions your customers ask you most. Include one about price.</>,
    <>Ask something deliberately vague, and watch whether it asks a good follow-up or guesses.</>,
    <>Ask the same question a second time. Answers vary slightly, and a question that only works sometimes needs a sharper source or a clearer instruction.</>,
    <>Select <code>Configure</code> from the top right, change one thing, and ask again.</>,
    <>Use <code>+ New chat</code> whenever you want a clean start with no history behind it.</>,
  ]}
  confirm={<>You have found at least one answer worth sharpening, changed one thing, and seen the difference.</>}
/>
</LabCallout>

Change one element at a time, whether that is the role, a capability, or a knowledge source. Changing three at once tells you nothing about which one worked.

## Put it on your website

The widget does not add itself. Installing the code is the step that turns a working configuration into a receptionist your visitors can reach, and it is also what connects the two halves of this lesson: a widget you install shows up back on the employee as a **trigger**, one of the events that invokes it.

<LabCallout title="create a widget you can embed">
Go to `Conversations` → `More` → `Conversations Settings`, on the **Web Chat** card. (`Administration` → `Conversations Settings` reaches the same page.)

<LabChecklist
  storageKey="turn-on-receptionist-lab-5"
  steps={[
    <>Select <code>Manage widgets</code> to open the <strong>Web Chat configuration</strong> page.</>,
    <>Select <code>New Web Chat</code> and give it a widget name. This creates a <strong>Standard</strong> widget, which is the kind you can embed.</>,
    <>On the <strong>AI employee</strong> card, select your receptionist.</>,
    <>Set the <strong>Welcome greeting</strong>, the <strong>Initial AI message</strong>, and the <strong>Appearance</strong> colors to match your brand, then save.</>,
  ]}
  confirm={<>Your widget is listed with its <code>Widget type</code> reading <strong>Standard</strong> and your receptionist assigned to it.</>}
/>
</LabCallout>

The widget that ships with your account has a `Widget type` of **System**, and its `⋮` menu offers only `Edit`. Embed codes live on Standard widgets, which is why the step above creates one.

<LabCallout title="embed it on your site">
Go to your new widget's `⋮` menu on the **Web Chat configuration** page.

<LabChecklist
  storageKey="turn-on-receptionist-lab-6"
  steps={[
    <>Choose <code>Embed</code>.</>,
    <>Open the <strong>Floating chat widget</strong> section and pick the tab that matches your site: <code>Website</code>, <code>Google Tag Manager</code>, <code>WordPress</code>, or <code>GoDaddy</code>.</>,
    <>Copy the code and paste it into your page head, just before the closing head tag, then publish.</>,
    <>Load your own site in an incognito window and start a conversation.</>,
  ]}
  confirm={<>The widget opens on your live site, the conversation appears in <code>Conversations</code>, and the contact is saved to your CRM.</>}
/>
</LabCallout>

Go back to the receptionist's `Configure` page and look at **Triggers**. Your widget is listed there now, as an event that invokes this employee, with its own `Settings`. That is the shape of the whole system: a channel arrives, a trigger fires, and the employee answers with the role, capabilities, and knowledge you just set.

:::tip Try it now
Open `Conversations` and find the chat you just had through your own widget. Whatever a visitor sends now lands here, and this is the inbox you will check in the morning.
:::

Everything deeper, voice configuration, custom capabilities, and building receptionists for clients in their Business App, has a path of its own: [Hire your first AI Employee](/learn/ai-workforce).

</LessonSection>
