## Configure yours

*You are here: Partner Center. Everything in this section happens in your workspace.*

Your receptionist serves your business, so it lives in your workspace. Open **AI** → **AI Workforce** and click **Configure** on the AI Chat Receptionist.

It works well out of the box. Give it a name and an image that fit your brand, and it will introduce itself accordingly. The capability that captures leads is on by default: it asks visitors for a name, phone number, and email, and saves what it gathers to your CRM. Add the capability that books appointments and connect it to a booking link from **My Meetings** in your CRM, and the receptionist offers real meeting times from your actual calendar. [This guide covers each setting](/ai/ai-workforce/ai-chat-receptionist/).

## Give it knowledge, sparingly

Knowledge is chosen, not dumped. Add your own website as a source, switch the mode to follow links so it learns every public page, and enable automatic refresh so it stays current on its own. Business facts that live nowhere on your site (hours, services, what makes you different) go in as plain text or file uploads.

Then stop. Less is more with AI knowledge: a lean, current set of sources keeps answers consistent, and you can add more the moment a real conversation shows a gap. [The Knowledge Base guide](/ai/knowledge-base/) covers every source type.

## Put it on your website

The widget does not add itself to your site; installing the code is what makes the receptionist real. In the widget setup, set the welcome greeting and match the colors to your brand, then copy the install code. One script works for most websites, with specific snippets for WordPress, GoDaddy, and Google Tag Manager. The floating widget is the strong default: it travels with the visitor down every page.

This is the exact setup you will later repeat for clients from their Business App.

## Put it through its paces

Configuration ends where testing begins. Click **Try it** on the receptionist's card to open a test page, and ask it what your customers ask you: services, hours, prices, something ambiguous, a meeting request. Watch whether it gives too much, too little, or exactly enough, then adjust its purpose, capabilities, or knowledge and ask again. If your receptionist also answers a phone line through the AI Voice Receptionist, call the number and run the same questions out loud.

Experimenting here is safe by design: any section you have edited is marked as modified and can be returned to its default.
