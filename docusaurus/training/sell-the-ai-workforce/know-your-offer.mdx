## The whole package

So what is a package? It is that whole team, pre-bundled and priced for one industry, sitting in the Marketplace ready to sell. Rather than assembling an offer product by product, you take a finished one and match it to the business in front of you.

Inside a package you get the products that carry the five agents: Conversations AI, Reputation AI, Social AI, and CRM AI, at the editions that industry needs. You also get a recommended retail price and a setup fee already filled in, plus the material to run the work once it is sold: sample AI Receptionist prompting scripts, a social calendar, email campaign templates, and plug-and-play automation workflows.

:::note Four or more pieces come with the AI Workforce

They appear in the recommended packages too, and each one is worth knowing for what it unlocks. Each has its own guide when you want the detail.

- **Local SEO.** Local keyword rank tracking, AI-built business profiles, and listing sync so the name, address, phone number, and hours stay accurate across 70 or more directories, including Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing, Yelp, and Facebook.
- **Campaigns Pro.** Automated email and SMS follow-up: seasonal offers, maintenance reminders, and referral asks, so repeat business does not depend on the owner remembering. [The campaigns guide](/marketing/campaigns) covers building and sending them.
- **Vibe.** An AI app builder you or your client can use to build a website, dashboard, or small app by describing what is needed in plain language. Included at the Standard edition. The package does not include a website outright, and Vibe gets a client most of the way there. [This guide covers what it can build](/ai/vibe).
- **Integrations.** More than 50 connections, and the ones that close deals are the vertical tools a prospect already runs: ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, and FieldEdge in home services; Shopmonkey, Tekmetric, and CCC ONE in automotive; PetExec, PawLoyalty, and Gingr in pet care; Clio in legal; QuickBooks in accounting; Salesforce and HubSpot in sales; Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom for scheduling; Zapier for everything else. [The full list lives here](https://docs.businessapp.io/business-app/administration/connections/).

:::

The integration list is worth knowing by heart for your own vertical. "We already run Jobber" is an objection until you can answer that the AI Receptionist books straight into it.

Contents vary by industry, so open the package you plan to sell and read its actual list before you quote it. That is the first thing you do below.

## One opening line, and the full script behind it

Every strong opener is the same move: name the outcome the owner already wants, phrase it as a question they answer yes to, and keep the technology out of it. The yes comes first; AI comes after.

The opener fits any local business, because the problems underneath it are universal: missed leads, slow response, an online presence that goes quiet after hours. Each industry below then carries the whole call: who you are talking to, the numbers that land, the seven discovery questions with their branches, the three-point pitch, the proof, the close, and the objections that industry actually raises. "Every time a potential customer reaches out and gets a voicemail or a generic 'we will get back to you' email, there is a good chance they are already clicking on your competitor's site. How much revenue is sitting in your voicemail box right now?"

</PitchSay>

Then confirm the pain, fast:

- Calls or messages go unanswered during your busiest hours?
- Reviews are hit or miss, rarely asked for?
- No real follow-up to keep customers coming back?

<PitchStage n={2} title="Discover the gap" />

Ask these in order. Each one has a branch and something to listen for.

1. "When someone calls or messages and nobody can respond right away, what happens to that inquiry right now?"
   - If voicemail: "How often do people actually leave a message, versus just reaching out to the next business on the list?"
   - If it just rings out: this is the money question, let it sit for a second.
   - Listening for: any admission that opportunities are being lost today.
2. "How many inquiries come in on a typical week, calls, messages, or web forms, and how many turn into actual customers?"
   - Listening for: a rough volume number to use later in the ROI math.
3. "How do you currently ask customers for reviews after they buy or use your service?"
   - If manual or "we do not, really": "So most transactions wrap up and nobody follows up for a review?"
   - Listening for: whether review generation is systematic or accidental.
4. "What do you do to stay in touch with past customers so they come back or refer someone?"
   - If nothing: "So repeat business is mostly luck right now, whoever happens to remember you?"
5. "How do you handle inquiries after hours or on weekends?"
   - If it goes to voicemail: "So anything outside business hours is a coin flip."
6. "What does a missed inquiry actually cost you, roughly, one sale, one project, one customer?"
   - Let them do the math out loud, do not supply the number yourself.
   - Listening for: a dollar figure you will reuse in the close.
7. "If every inquiry got a fast response, even during your busiest hours, would that be worth a few minutes?"
   - A "yes" here is the bridge straight into the pitch below.

:::

"Third, be the one they keep coming back to, once the sale is made or the job is done, review requests go out automatically, and follow-up campaigns keep customers coming back."

</PitchSay>

If they push back on the numbers, run their answers to questions 2 and 6 through the calculator right there. A real projected number beats a mental estimate.

**Objections in this industry**

| Objection | Your response |
|---|---|
| "My business is too small or too niche for something like this." | It scales to your volume, not the other way around. One recovered inquiry usually covers the cost. |
| "I do not get enough calls or messages to justify it." | Even a low-volume business loses real revenue to the handful it misses, and those are often the highest-intent ones. |
| "I do not want to lose the personal touch." | It only covers what you cannot get to. You are still the one who builds the relationship once it is in front of you. |
| "This sounds expensive, what is the ROI for a business my size?" | Think of it against the cost of one missed inquiry, you told me that is about [their number]. This pays for itself the first one it recovers. |
| "I already have someone answering calls." | It covers what they cannot, nights, weekends, lunch, when they are already on another call. |
| "I am skeptical of AI in general, it will sound robotic." | It is built on your actual business information, so it sounds like your business, not a generic bot. |
| "I have tried automation or chatbots before and it did not work." | This is not a pop-up chatbot, it answers real calls and messages the way a person would, and hands off anything it cannot handle. |
| "I do not have a 'pipeline.' I just sell stuff." | This is not really about "leads" or a "pipeline," it is about making sure the person who wants to buy from you right now does not get frustrated and leave. Think of it like having an extra set of hands on the floor when the shop gets busy. |

**Multi-location variant.** Swap the close for "Worth a short call with your ops lead to see this live across a couple of locations?" Ask question 6 as "what does a missed inquiry cost across your locations in a bad month?" since multi-location buyers think in aggregate, not per call. In the pitch step, swap "your team" for "every location," pushing the consistency angle harder, one process across every location instead of five homegrown habits. Multi-location objections also skew toward vendor management and consistency across locations rather than personal affordability, so redirect the "cannot afford it" response toward a per-location or aggregate ROI number.
