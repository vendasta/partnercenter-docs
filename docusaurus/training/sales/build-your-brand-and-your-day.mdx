## Decide who you serve, and tell their story

A brand is a set of decisions, not a logo. Four of them do most of the work: who you serve, what those clients can expect, whose story your copy tells, and whether your operation keeps the promise. Expand each one:

          <p><strong>Global means niching down.</strong> Selling online, a tight vertical (just dentists, just med spas) lets you build specific messaging and offer real territory exclusivity, because your clients are scattered nationally and never compete.</p>

            <li>Decide what you want them to expect from you, and mean it. If you want a monthly standing call, say so.</li>

## Structure your selling day

A producing day is a set of habits, not a schedule. These six separate the reps whose day generates conversations from the reps whose day generates admin. Expand each one:

<Accordion
  items={[
    {
      id: 'worst-first',
      title: 'Attack the thing you are avoiding, first',
      badge: '1',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>The cancellation call, the customer who owes money, the twenty-page proposal. Left alone they sit in the back of your mind and show up in your voice on every call in between, because customers can tell when you are carrying something heavy. Clear it before you touch anything else.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'minutes',
      title: 'The minutes add up',
      badge: '2',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>You can get more budget, more headcount, more product. You cannot get more time. Track where a normal day actually goes. Then protect your calendar the way a dentist's office protects theirs: they call nine times before an appointment because an empty chair costs them money.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'numbers',
      title: 'Become a chemist about your own numbers',
      badge: '3',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Month-end revenue is a <em>lag</em> measure. By the time you see it, it is too late to change it.</p>
          <p>Act on the <em>leading</em> measures: calls made, proposals out, actual closing ratio. Know your close rate is 20% and you need $100,000, and you can work backward to how many live opportunities the pipeline needs. Aim for about 120% of that as a buffer.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'sixty-thirty-ten',
      title: 'The 60/30/10 rule',
      badge: '4',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>60% talking to customers, 30% getting better at your craft, 10% administration. Most reps who feel busy without hitting budget have it flipped: heavy on admin, light on actual conversations.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'line-by-line',
      title: 'Master the line-by-line',
      badge: '5',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Without opening a spreadsheet, can you talk through your top ten accounts: what is happening, why it matters, when you last spoke, and when you speak next? If not, you do not know your book as well as you think. The spreadsheet organizes it. The line-by-line proves you know it.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'next-opportunity',
      title: 'Always be looking for the next opportunity',
      badge: '6',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>"The way we have always done it" is a liability here, not a cliché. A rep certain they have found the ceiling is the rep who gets outworked by someone hungrier.</p>
        </>
      )
    }
  ]}
/>

## Split your book into A, B, and C

Not every account deserves the same hour. Flip each tier to see what it gets from you:

<IconFlipCardGrid>
  <IconFlipCard gradient="blue" front="A accounts" back="Protect: Paying you real money right now. When an A account raises a hand, you drop what you are doing." />
  <IconFlipCard gradient="green" front="B accounts" back="Invest: Your biggest opportunities, or A accounts in the making. This is where extra selling effort goes." />
  <IconFlipCard gradient="purple" front="C accounts" back="Limit: Flat or going nowhere. Find the one thing that would grow them, or manage them out." />
</IconFlipCardGrid>

C accounts include fallen angels, former A accounts where something changed. Either find the specific thing that grows one back into a B, or manage it out. Aim to spend roughly 80% of your time on A accounts and the B accounts becoming A accounts.

## Proof from the field

Two of Vendasta's top-performing partner agencies were asked what separates them. Neither credited a tactic. One runs a standing monthly performance review so the client never wonders whether it is working: *"if you're not proving value, they're going to start shaking when they write that check."* The other was blunter: *"no lotion, potion, no magic. Just not afraid to work. First one in the building, last one to leave."*

</LessonSection>

:::
