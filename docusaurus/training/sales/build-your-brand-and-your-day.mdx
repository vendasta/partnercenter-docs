## A day that produces conversations

Two reps can work the same hours and end the month in completely different places. The difference is rarely talent. It is that one of them ran their day, and the other one answered it.

A producing day is a set of habits, not a schedule you fill in. Eight of them separate the reps whose hours turn into conversations from the reps whose hours turn into admin.

Sales expert George Leith walks through all eight. Watch it first, then work through them one at a time below.

### The three tiers, at a glance

<FlipCardGrid>
  <FlipCard front="A accounts" back="Paying you real money right now. When an A account raises a hand, you drop what you are doing." subtext="Protect" size="large" />
  <FlipCard front="B accounts" back="Your biggest opportunities, or A accounts in the making. This is where extra selling effort goes." subtext="Invest" size="large" />
  <FlipCard front="C accounts" back="Flat or going nowhere. Find the one thing that would grow them, or manage them out." subtext="Limit" size="large" />
</FlipCardGrid>

C accounts include fallen angels: former A accounts where something changed, a buyer left, or the season turned. Either find the specific thing that grows one back into a B, or manage it out. Aim to spend roughly 80% of your time on A accounts and the B accounts becoming A accounts.

## Zoom out: the positioning behind the day

A well-run day still needs something worth selling to someone specific. Four decisions do most of that work, and none of them are a logo.

**Who you serve depends on how far you can reach.**

| Your reach | What fits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Local | One or two verticals with enough variety that no two clients compete | A roofer and an HVAC company, not two roofers three blocks apart. You are building relationships with community leaders, and that breaks if anyone suspects you also sell to their rival. |
| Global | A tight niche, such as just dentists or just med spas | Selling online, your clients are scattered nationally and never compete, so you can build specific messaging and offer real territory exclusivity. |

Same skill set either way. The variable is reach, not talent.

**Build the profile from your best clients, not your average ones.** List what your genuinely good clients have in common: they pay on time, take your advice, and treat you as a partner. Work out where they spend time and money. Decide what you want them to expect from you and mean it, because if you promise a monthly standing call you now owe one. Then name what has to change in your own operation to deliver it.

**Then make the client the hero.** Nobody's problem gets solved by your tenure.

<Admonition type="tip" icon="✓" title="Rewriting a homepage line">
  <p><strong>Before:</strong> "We've been in business 10 years. Our team of experts handles your digital marketing."</p>
  <p><strong>After:</strong> "Local businesses struggle to get found online. You have a plan, we help you execute it. Here's what it looks like when your phone rings with qualified leads."</p>
  <p>The prospect should see themselves in the first sentence, not your company history.</p>
</Admonition>

A compelling story earns the first conversation. What keeps the account is whether your operation delivers what the story promised: repeatable systems, reliable follow-through, and professionalism a client notices even when nothing has gone wrong. If you will guide someone through fixing their business, run the same audit on your own.

## Proof from the field

Two of Vendasta's top-performing partner agencies were asked what separates them. Neither credited a tactic. One runs a standing monthly performance review so the client never wonders whether it is working: *"if you're not proving value, they're going to start shaking when they write that check."* The other was blunter: *"no lotion, potion, no magic. Just not afraid to work. First one in the building, last one to leave."*

:::tip Try it now
Two parts. First, write the "before and after" of your own website's opening line, and decide who the hero is in each version. Second, do a line-by-line out loud, no spreadsheet, on your top five accounts. Wherever you go blank is this week's work.
:::

## Let your AI Workforce carry the 10%

The habit hardest to protect is the 60% that should go to customers, because admin quietly eats it.

<Admonition type="info" icon="✓" title="AI Sales Assistant — keeps the record without your evening">
  <p>After a meeting is recorded and analyzed, it updates the CRM records from that conversation and turns action items into activities, so a commitment you made out loud becomes something on a list without you retyping it. You can also ask about your book in plain language, such as "What opportunities are currently open in my pipeline?", which is the line-by-line habit with the spreadsheet step removed. It is part of CRM AI and needs a CRM AI Standard or Pro subscription; [the AI Sales Assistant guide](/ai/ai-workforce/ai-sales-assistant) covers what it updates and how to control it.</p>
</Admonition>

## Where this leaves you

A perfect sales day is not a heroic one. It is the hard thing done first, protected hours, numbers you actually watch, and a book of business sorted by what each account deserves.

Get the day repeatable and the only question left is what you put into it. That starts with having someone to call.
