## An objection is information, not a no

An objection almost never means what it sounds like on the surface. *"I don't know you"* means "reassure me." *"It's not my decision"* means "I do not want to decide alone." *"I need to think about it"* means "I have doubts I have not said out loud."

Every one of them is a request for information, not a locked door. The reps who close more are not the ones who avoid objections. They are the ones with a process for working through them.

Two short videos cover this. The first names the five objections you will hear most and what each one is really telling you.

## The five objections you will hear most

Almost every push-back is one of five. Naming which one you are facing tells you what to say back.

<Accordion
  items={[
    {
      id: 'price',
      title: 'Price: "I can find something cheaper"',
      badge: '1',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They do not see the value yet. "Too expensive" is usually code for "not worth what you are charging." Highlight the long-term savings, break the cost into smaller units so the number stops feeling abstract, and offer proof of customers who came out ahead. Reaching for a discount just confirms the price was padded.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Ask "too expensive compared to what?" to find out whether they are benchmarking you against a rival or against nothing.</li>
            <li>Then ask whether it is a cash flow issue or a budget issue, because those need different answers.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'authority',
      title: 'Authority: "I need to ask my boss"',
      badge: '2',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They do not feel confident deciding alone. Often a stall rather than a lie, and challenging it creates conflict without moving anything. Keep the process going instead: <em>"I absolutely understand. Could we set up a meeting with the other decision-makers?"</em></p>
          <ul>
            <li>Ask what objections they will face internally, then offer to build their business case for them.</li>
            <li>Ask to be in the room where the decision happens, rather than handing over a summary and waiting.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'trust',
      title: 'Trust: "I only work with brands I know"',
      badge: '3',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They need reassurance, not a defense. Demonstrate capability by knowing your solution cold, show integrity with real examples of putting the customer first, and give objective proof: case studies, testimonials, and references rather than your own word.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Give a count plus one name: "we work with 15 companies like yours, including X."</li>
            <li>If they raise a bad review, state what you changed because of it rather than explaining it away.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'complacency',
      title: 'Complacency: "I\'m fine with how things are"',
      badge: '4',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>The status quo is comfortable and what you sell is not urgent yet. Name the specific changes that come from saying yes, then build urgency: the industry is moving, competitors are adapting, and standing still has a cost.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Ask "how are you solving for that today?" so the prospect names the gap instead of you.</li>
            <li>Remove the risk of choosing wrong, which is usually the real blocker, rather than pushing harder.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'competitor',
      title: 'Competitor: "We already have someone for that"',
      badge: '5',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Good news. They already believe they need this, they just do not think it comes from you. Ask about their experience with the incumbent, let them name the gaps themselves, then show how you differ on those specific points. Competitor-bashing reads as petty and costs you credibility.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Ask what they like best about working with them, then stay quiet and let them keep going.</li>
            <li>Get the renewal date, then ask how they would move forward if the contract did not exist.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    }
  ]}

  items={[
    {
      id: 'curiosity',
      title: 'Express curiosity',
      badge: '1',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Answer an objection with a question rather than a rebuttal, so the prospect explains themselves and surfaces the real issue on their own. People like explaining their reasoning, and the explanation is usually more useful than the objection was.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Avoid opening with "why", which sounds like a challenge. Try "can you help me understand what's driving that?"</li>
            <li>Ask something that cannot be answered yes or no, so there is somewhere to go next.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'conditional',
      title: 'Offer a conditional close',
      badge: '2',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Make a social agreement in a fixed shape: "if I do this, will you agree to that." It ties the objection directly to the solution, so a yes to the condition is a soft yes to the sale.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Lead with the "if I can" half, since opening on "will you" invites a fresh objection first.</li>
            <li>Isolate before you condition: "aside from that, is anything else holding this up?"</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'reframe',
      title: 'Reframe the objection',
      badge: '3',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Agree with the fact, then change what it means. You are not arguing the point, you are shifting the lens from negative to positive: <em>"Yes, the cost is higher, and that is a testament to how it is built."</em></p>
          <ul>
            <li>Ask "can I bounce a few thoughts off you?" rather than "can I make a suggestion?", which invites defensiveness.</li>
            <li>Close the loop afterwards: "what part of that still feels unresolved?" stops false agreement resurfacing later.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'boomerang',
      title: 'Come back with a boomerang',
      badge: '4',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Repeat the objection back, then make it the very reason to buy. <em>"I get that you're busy. That is exactly why this is worth it, because it takes five hours a week off you."</em></p>
          <ul>
            <li>The hinge phrase is "that's exactly why", which turns the objection into your evidence.</li>
            <li>Always attach a real proof point. A bare boomerang reads as brushing the concern aside.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'feel-felt-found',
      title: 'Show empathy with feel, felt, found',
      badge: '5',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Take three steps that put a prospect at ease: show you understand how they feel, note that others felt the same, then tell them what those others found once they went ahead. Validate, normalize, resolve.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>Say "some people feel" rather than "I understand how you feel", which invites "no you don't".</li>
            <li>Put real names and numbers in the "found" step, because a vague outcome exposes the script.</li>
          </ul>
        </>
      )
    }

## Work the sequence before you answer

Neither video names an order of operations, so here is one worth borrowing. LAIR runs before you reach for any specific response, and the sequence matters more than any single line inside it.

| Step | What it means | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **L**: Listen | Let them finish, uninterrupted. Do not jump to a rebuttal. | On the phone, "mm-hmm" or "I see" rather than dead silence. |
| **A**: Acknowledge | Tell them the concern is valid, then probe for what sits under it. | "It sounds like you're worried about X. Can you tell me more?" |
| **I**: Identify | Repeat the concern back in your own words and confirm you have it right. | "So it's the contract length, not the price itself?" |
| **R**: Redirect | Respond with a question first, then the answer, then the proof. | "Can I bounce a few thoughts off you?" |

:::tip Try it now
Take an objection you heard this week. Name which of the five it was, then run it through LAIR out loud. Now pick one bounce-back technique you did not use in the moment and rewrite your response with it.
:::
