## An objection is information, not a no

An objection almost never means what it sounds like on the surface. *"I don't know you"* means "reassure me." *"It's not my decision"* means "I do not want to decide alone." *"I need to think about it"* means "I have doubts I have not said out loud."

Every one of them is a request for information, not a locked door. The reps who close more are not the ones who avoid objections. They are the ones with a process for working through them.

LAIR is a repeatable order of operations for any objection, before you know which specific one you are facing. The sequence matters more than any single line inside it. Expand each one:

<Accordion
  items={[
    {
      id: 'listen',
      title: 'Listen',
      badge: 'L',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Let the prospect finish, uninterrupted. Do not jump to a rebuttal. On the phone, signal you are there with a "mm-hmm" or "I see" rather than silence.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'acknowledge',
      title: 'Acknowledge and clarify',
      badge: 'A',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Tell them the concern is valid, then probe if you suspect more underneath it: <em>"It sounds like you're concerned about X. Can you tell me more?"</em></p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'identify',
      title: 'Identify',
      badge: 'I',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Repeat the concern back in your own words and confirm you have it right. This is the step that proves you were listening rather than waiting for your turn.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'redirect',
      title: 'Redirect',
      badge: 'R',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Respond first with a <em>question</em>, not an answer. Only once the prospect is out of defensive mode will they hear your solution. Then redirect with context, data, or a customer story that speaks to the specific doubt.</p>
        </>

    }

## The five objections you will hear most

Almost every push-back is one of five: price, authority, trust, complacency, or a competitor. Naming which one you are facing tells you what to say back, and each opens with the words you will actually hear. Expand each one:

<Accordion
  items={[
    {
      id: 'price',
      title: 'Price: "I can find something cheaper"',
      badge: '1',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They do not see the value yet. "Too expensive" is usually code for "not worth what you're charging." Do not reach for a discount, which just confirms the price was inflated. Highlight long-term savings, break the cost into smaller units so the number feels less abstract, and offer proof of customers who came out ahead.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'authority',
      title: 'Authority: "I need to ask my boss"',
      badge: '2',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They do not feel confident deciding alone. Often a stall rather than a lie, but do not challenge it even if you suspect so, because that creates conflict without moving anything. Keep the process going: <em>"I absolutely understand. Could we set up a group meeting with the other decision-makers?"</em></p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'trust',
      title: 'Trust: "I only work with brands I know"',
      badge: '3',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>They need reassurance, not a defense. Demonstrate capability by knowing your solution cold, show integrity with real examples of putting the customer first, and give objective proof: case studies, testimonials, references, not just your word.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'complacency',
      title: 'Complacency: "I\'m fine with how things are"',
      badge: '4',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>The status quo is comfortable and what you sell is not urgent yet. Name the specific changes that come from saying yes, then build urgency: the industry is moving, competitors are adapting, and standing still has a cost.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'competitor',
      title: 'Competitor: "We already have someone for that"',
      badge: '5',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Good news. They already believe they need this, they just do not think it comes from you. Ask about their experience with the incumbent (<em>"What's working? What isn't?"</em>), let them name the gaps themselves, then show how you differ on those specific points. Competitor-bashing reads as petty and costs you credibility.</p>
        </>
      )
    }
  ]}
/>

These five are not tied to any one objection. Reach for whichever fits the moment, or combine more than one when the conversation calls for it. Expand each one:

<Accordion
  items={[
    {
      id: 'curiosity',
      title: 'Express curiosity',
      badge: '1',
      content: (
        <>
          <p><em>"Out of curiosity, why doesn't this work for you?"</em> People like explaining themselves, and the act of explaining often surfaces the real issue.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'conditional',
      title: 'Offer a conditional close',
      badge: '2',
      content: (
        <>
          <p><em>"If I can show you this saves you money, can we keep talking?"</em> A yes to the condition is a soft yes to the sale.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'reframe',
      title: 'Reframe the objection',
      badge: '3',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Agree first, then shift the lens: <em>"Yes, the colour's unusual, and that is exactly what makes it memorable."</em> Do not argue the fact. Change what it means.</p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'boomerang',
      title: 'Come back with a boomerang',
      badge: '4',
      content: (
        <>
          <p>Repeat the objection, then make it the reason to buy: <em>"You're right, you are busy. That is exactly why this saves you five hours a week."</em></p>
        </>
      )
    },
    {
      id: 'feel-felt-found',
      title: 'Show empathy with feel, felt, found',
      badge: '5',
      content: (
        <>
          <p><em>"I understand how you feel. Other customers felt the same way. Once they got started, they found [the actual outcome]."</em> Validate, normalize, resolve.</p>
        </>
      )
    }
  ]}
/>
