## Stop opening with the weather

Fifteen minutes of small talk assumes a business owner has fifteen minutes to spare. Get to why they are on the call, and prove you are listening a different way: paraphrase what they just told you before you move on.

:::info Say it back
"So if I have got this right, the real issue is [X], and it is costing you [Y]. Is that a fair read?"
:::

That one sentence builds more trust than twenty minutes of weather, because it proves you were listening instead of waiting for your turn.

## The three questions that do most of the work

Asked in order, these uncover almost everything you need.

## Discover, then define

Two moves, not one. First **discover**: ask and listen without jumping to a solution. Then **define**: say back what you heard and get them to confirm you understood the real challenge, not just the first thing mentioned. Only after "yes, that's it" should the conversation move to what you recommend.

Going straight from discover to pitch is how you solve the wrong problem confidently.

## Come in with a hypothesis, not a script

Do not walk in with a blank page. A Snapshot Report gives you a starting read across listings, reviews, social, website, SEO, and advertising, so you arrive with an idea of where the gaps probably are. Cover their business and what they sell, what they use today, where it falls short in their words, and how much they already know about what you offer.

The report is a hypothesis. The call is where you find out whether it is right, so do not read it *at* them instead of asking.

## Tell them when something does not fit

The strongest trust move on a discovery call is sometimes telling a prospect that something is not for them. It costs an add-on now and buys proof that you solve for their outcome rather than your quota. A prospect who hears "that one is probably not worth it for you" believes you completely the next time you say something is.

## Match your depth to the opportunity

Someone who knows exactly what they want does not need you to dig for a hidden strategic problem. Give them fast service and respect that they did their own discovery. Someone building a brand or fixing something they cannot quite name is where the three questions earn their keep. Running every call the same way wastes effort on the simple ones and shortchanges the complex ones.

:::tip Try it now
On your next discovery call, open with only the three questions, nothing else prepared. Paraphrase each answer back before you say anything else. Notice how much of the call you do not have to script once you have heard the real answer.
:::
