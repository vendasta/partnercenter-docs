Your profile is usually the first thing a prospect sees about you. Five things matter more than the rest.

## The score that tells you if you are actually doing this

LinkedIn's Social Selling Index is a checklist, not a vanity number. It scores how complete and active your personal brand is, how well you find the right people, how often you engage with insight rather than likes, and how much your relationships develop over time. A low score is not a mystery. It names which of the four you are skipping.

## Research them, because they are already researching you

Ten minutes before every meeting: who you are meeting, what they post about, what you have in common. Most reps know that half.

The half people forget is that the prospect is very likely doing the same thing to you before the same meeting. A thin profile does not merely fail to help, it costs you credibility before you have said a word. Your own profile is part of your prep, not a setup task you finished two years ago.

## Share what makes you the expert, not the salesperson

Make it a daily routine rather than a quarterly chore: check connection requests, comment on a couple of posts, share one useful thing. What performs is what helps people learn, not another product announcement.

You do not need to be the original author to be the person who finds and understands the good stuff first. When you read something genuinely good from a source your network trusts, share it with two or three sentences of your own take up front. That habit is far easier to sustain than writing from a blank page.

:::tip Try it now
Open your own LinkedIn profile and check three things: does your headline say what you do and why someone should care, is your featured section filled in, and do your top two claimed skills actually have endorsements? Fix whichever is weakest today.
:::
