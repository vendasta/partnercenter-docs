## The pitch you did not earn

A pitch is something you can write before you have met the prospect. A recommendation is something you can only write after, and a prospect can tell which one they are getting.

Three agency owners on a panel about sales automation landed on the same rule without prompting each other: never automate the actual sales conversation. That conversation is the one place you find out what the client needs, and a recommendation built on a guess is a pitch wearing a nicer outfit.

:::info The whole job in one sentence
"This is your chance to understand what the client needs. And after you know what the client needs, this allows you to then map the problem and how you can best solve that."
:::

Understand, then map, then recommend. Skip the first step and you are guessing out loud.

## Why trust beats features

Roughly three of every four dollars small businesses spend on software and services flow through an expert they already trust, not the vendor with the longest feature list. B2B buyers are also more emotionally attached to the providers they rely on than typical consumers are to the brands they buy.

That is not a reason to be less rigorous about your product. It is why the questions above matter more than your deck. A prospect is not deciding whether your product is good. They are deciding whether you are the person whose recommendation they can trust without checking it themselves.

## Keep consulting after the sale

The same rule applies to reporting. An automated monthly report is fine. What keeps a client is walking them through their real numbers yourself, in a short screen recording or a call. Skip the vanity metrics and narrate the ones that show where the business improved.

Clients who get that walkthrough trust the relationship more, because they can tell you are not hiding behind a dashboard when the numbers are less than perfect.

:::tip Try it now
Before your next consultation, write down the three homework questions above and actually get answers before the call, even if it is just a two-line email. Then, in the call itself, ask the two follow-up questions before you say a single word about what you would recommend.
:::
