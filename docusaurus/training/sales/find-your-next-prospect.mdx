## A full funnel beats a big deal

You have seen it happen: someone crushes every record one month and is scraping the bottom of the leaderboard the next. It is almost never that they got worse at selling. It is that they stopped prospecting while they were busy servicing the deals they just won, and the funnel behind them went empty.

Prospecting is not the thing you do when you have spare time. It is the thing that decides whether you have a next month at all. Ten habits are what keep it full.

Sales expert George Leith walks through all ten. Watch it first, then work through them one at a time below.

:::tip Two numbers worth remembering
- **~4 seconds** is the average human attention span, a shade behind a goldfish's five. Lead with the headline, every time.
- **60x** is how much more likely a prospect is to convert if you reach them within 60 seconds of opening your message.
:::

## Make your profile a prospecting asset

Habits 7, 8, and 9 all run through the same place: the profile a prospect finds when they look you up. Five things carry most of the weight.

| Profile element | What makes it work |
|---|---|
| Photo and banner | A professional headshot, not the beach photo with your dog. Use the banner for your brand or a recent win. |
| Headline | Say why someone should care, not just your title. "Helping local businesses fix the gaps in their online presence" beats "Account Executive." |
| Summary | The highest-leverage 200 words you have. Tell a story rather than restating your resume. |
| Featured section | Link your best work. Most people leave this empty, which is exactly why filling it in separates you. |
| Recommendations and skills | At least one recommendation from someone who actually worked with you, and endorsements behind any skill you claim. |

LinkedIn's Social Selling Index scores four things: how complete your presence is, how well you find the right people, whether you engage with insight rather than likes, and how your relationships develop over time. Treat a low score as a checklist of what you are skipping, not as a vanity metric.

## Automate the outreach grind

Everything above is a discipline. The next part is a tool. A sales engagement platform like Yesware plugs into Gmail or Outlook and takes over the mechanical side of outreach, so you spend your time on conversations instead of the busywork around them:

- **Track** — get a real-time notification when a prospect opens your email or clicks a link, so you know exactly when to follow up. This is the respond-fast habit, automated.
- **Search** — pull verified contact data (phone, email, social) for new leads in a couple of clicks instead of hours of manual research.
- **Standardize** — build reusable, personalizable email templates once, instead of rewriting the same email every time.
- **Automate** — schedule multi-touch outreach across several days, so the fourth and fifth touches actually get sent instead of quietly forgotten.
- **Analyze** — see which campaigns, cadences, and follow-up counts actually produce replies, so you stop guessing.

None of this replaces the habits above. It removes the manual grind around them so more of your day goes to actually talking to people, which is where the 60/30/10 rule says most of your time belongs anyway.

:::tip Try it now
Pick one prospect you have not followed up with this week. Write the headline version of your outreach — one sentence of value, one question — before you write anything longer.
:::

## Put your AI Workforce on the funnel

Two AI Employees do real work around prospecting, and it is worth being precise about which part each one takes.

<Admonition type="info" icon="✓" title="AI Chat Receptionist — works the top of the funnel">
  <p>Answers website visitors around the clock using your knowledge base and website content, and collects a name, phone number, or email so you have someone to follow up with. That is the respond-fast habit running without you: someone is engaged right now, and something is responding right now instead of tomorrow morning. When you are ready to put one on your site, [this guide walks you through setup](/ai/ai-workforce/ai-chat-receptionist/).</p>
</Admonition>

<Admonition type="info" icon="✓" title="AI Sales Assistant — works behind the funnel">
  <p>After a meeting is recorded and analyzed, it updates the CRM records that came out of that conversation and creates activities from the action items, so a commitment you made out loud becomes something on a list. Ask it about your pipeline in plain language, such as "What opportunities are currently open in my pipeline?", instead of building a report. It is part of CRM AI and needs a CRM AI Standard or Pro subscription; [the AI Sales Assistant guide](/ai/ai-workforce/ai-sales-assistant) covers what it updates and how to control it.</p>
</Admonition>

Both work on people already in your world: a visitor on your site, a contact in your CRM, a meeting that already happened. An automation can even place an outbound AI call to a contact you already hold. What none of them do is find someone who has never heard of you, and the ten habits above are how you do that.

## Where this leaves you

Prospecting is not a burst of activity after a slow month. It is ten small habits running daily, a profile that holds up when someone looks you up, and the tools and AI Employees that take the mechanical work off your plate.

Keep the funnel full and everything downstream gets easier. What changes next is what you actually say when one of those prospects picks up.
