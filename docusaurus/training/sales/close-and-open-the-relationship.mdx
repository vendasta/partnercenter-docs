## "Close" is the wrong word

The account you just opened is worth more over three years than the deal you just signed is worth today, and the first 90 days decide which one you get.

The economics are blunt. If every client you sign fills the seat of someone who churned last month, you are running in place. Growth needs the new seat to be a net-new seat, so a renewal is worth more to your number than a new logo. It does not cost you anything to win it twice.

## Check in at 30, 60, and 90 days

On a schedule you set, not one they have to ask for.

## Catch the one-degree drift

A plane one degree off course is nothing in the first few minutes and lands hundreds of miles away by the end of the flight. A small unmet expectation is cheap to fix in week two and expensive in month five. Say the quiet part out loud at each check-in, such as what has not happened yet and the plan to get there, instead of hoping nobody notices.

Write yourself a one-page playbook per check-in before you need it: what to pull, what to ask, and what to do with a "this part isn't working" answer. That is not a robotic script. It is making sure your 30-day check-in is as good on your worst week as your best one.

## At-risk is not the same as lost

A client is *at risk* when something could end the relationship. A client has *churned* when the revenue is gone. Treating those as one event is how reps give up early: an at-risk client is still a client, and usually one who will tell you exactly what is wrong if you ask.

The reason is usually not your work. It is more often financial pressure, a change in their business, or something human. The fix for "their budget got cut" is a different conversation than "we dropped the ball in month two," and you cannot tell which without asking.

## When you do lose one, lose it well

You cannot save them all. What you control is how it ends. Be efficient about the wind-down, do not make them chase you for the last steps, and say plainly that the door is open.

:::info Ending it cleanly
"I'm sorry this didn't work out the way we planned. I'll get everything wrapped up cleanly on our end this week. If your situation changes down the road, I'd genuinely like to work together again, call me directly."
:::

Budgets come back and owners move to new companies. A client you lost gracefully is a lead. A client you lost badly is a review you cannot undo.

:::tip Try it now
Pick a client you closed in the last 90 days. Write down what you promised them in the pitch, one sentence. Then write down whether you have already shown them, in numbers, that it happened. If you haven't, that's this week's check-in.
:::
