This step gives you the distinction the rest of this path is built on: what generative AI actually is, and how it differs from every other tool you and your clients have ever used. Six short steps from here, you will be able to explain [AI Employees](/ai) from the inside out. There is nothing to set up; this whole path runs on plain language.

## Two kinds of software

Every tool your clients used before now follows rules. It classifies, routes, and selects from outcomes someone defined in advance. A booking form checks a calendar. A filter sends messages with "urgent" in the subject to the front of the queue. Give it the same input twice and it does the same thing twice. That predictability is its strength, and its ceiling: it can only ever do what it was built to do.

Generative AI works differently. It produces new content by predicting what comes next, based on patterns learned from enormous amounts of text. It does not select from predefined outcomes; it generates one, shaped by the situation in front of it.

One example makes the difference concrete. A rule can route an email containing the word "invoice" to the bookkeeper, every time, instantly. Generative AI can read the email and draft the reply: a response that did not exist anywhere before, fitted to what the sender actually asked.

## Rule-shaped and judgment-shaped work

That distinction gives you a practical sorting tool. Every task in a business is one of two shapes:

- **Rule-shaped work** has fixed logic. Same trigger, same steps, same result, every time. Sending a receipt, routing a message, updating a record.
- **Judgment-shaped work** needs interpretation. Every instance is a little different: answering a customer's question in their own words, qualifying a lead, writing a response to a review.

Software has handled rule-shaped work for decades. Generative AI is the first technology that handles judgment-shaped work at scale. That is the entire shift, in one sentence.

:::tip Try it now
Think of the most repetitive task in your week. Ask one question about it: does it follow fixed rules, or does each instance need a little judgment? Keep your answer; the final step of this path comes back to it.
:::

## From tools you operate to a workforce you direct

You met the platform's mental model in Get started: **AI does the work, you orchestrate.** This path is why that model works.

Software used to be something you operated. You clicked, it responded, and the output was only ever as good as the hands on the keyboard. A workforce is different: you tell it what outcome you want, give it what it needs to do the job well, and review the results. AI Employees answer the calls and messages; your judgment decides what they know, what they are allowed to do, and what good looks like.

That last part matters most. The AI is not the differentiator; it is available to everyone. Your judgment about where to apply it, and the client knowledge you feed it, is the product.

## What your clients need from you

Your clients hear AI noise everywhere: headlines, sales pitches, and big promises. What they need is someone who can tell them, in plain language, what it is genuinely good at, where it needs direction, and what that means for their business. By the end of this path, that someone is you. Each of the next five steps hands you one piece of the vocabulary: how AI Employees think, what they know, what they do, how they act, and when a workflow beats a worker.
