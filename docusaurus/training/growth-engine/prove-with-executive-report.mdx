## The report that keeps the client

The Snapshot Report won the deal. It is the wrong tool for keeping it.

The split is clean: **the Snapshot Report is for prospecting, before you have a relationship. The Executive Report is for after.** One grades a stranger's online presence to start a conversation; the other reports what your work actually produced, month after month, under your brand.

Northside Dental signed because a report showed them where they stood. They will renew because a second report shows them how far they have moved.

The number worth knowing: Business App users who receive a weekly or monthly Executive Report have a **38% higher chance of being retained** than those who do not. Not because the report is persuasive, but because a client who sees the work is a client who remembers paying for a reason.

## What is in it, and where it comes from

The Executive Report pulls metrics from the products active on the account into one rollup — Leads, Website, Reputation, Social, Listings, SEO, Advertising, and Accounting. It is white-labelled under your brand, mobile-responsive, and **needs no sign-in to view**, which is why it works as an email rather than a login chore.

Personalized data starts appearing within about five minutes of running a Snapshot Report or authenticating Google Business Profile on the account.

## When it sends, and when it does not

That last rule is the one clients ask about. A section that has not moved simply does not appear, and on accounts where **only Local SEO** is active, no data change means no report at all. It is working as designed, but it looks like a bug from the client's side, so it is worth knowing before the phone call.

:::warning Deactivating products does not stop the emails
Report scheduling persists until someone disables it explicitly. Turn it off per user with **Edit Notifications** → **Business App**, or in bulk from **Accounts** → **Manage Users** → **Bulk Update**. And if a client hits Unsubscribe by accident, that cannot be self-served — it takes a support request to put them back on.
:::

## Shape the report before the client sees it

Which sections appear, and in what order, is yours to set at **Partner Center** → **Accounts** → **Manage Business App** → **Customize Business App** → **Executive Report** → **Sections**.

Pick the market from the dropdown in the top right first, because each market holds its own configuration. Then toggle sections on or off, and drag them into the order you want. The same rule from reading the Snapshot applies here: lead with what you can talk about.

Four constraints to plan around:

- **One order serves both frequencies.** You cannot have a different section order for weekly and monthly.
- **Changes apply to historical reports too**, not just future ones.
- **There is no preview.** The only way to see it is to be a recipient, so add yourself as a user on a test account.
- **Allow up to 24 hours** for changes to reach scheduled reports.

Turning off **Show this page** removes the tab from Business App, but email delivery can continue based on notification settings — the two are separate switches.

## Custom date ranges, and the two cards that vanish

The report defaults to weekly or monthly, and you can select a fully custom range from the top right.

One thing to expect when you do: **Listings and Social AI cards are not available on a custom date range.** Those sources arrive in fixed monthly rollups and cannot be sliced arbitrarily. A banner appears in the app to say so, and switching back to the standard weekly or monthly view brings them back. If a client reports missing cards, ask what date range they are on before anything else.

## The renewal conversation

The report is the meeting. Run it the way you ran the Snapshot: not as a recital of every number, but as a story with a direction.

- **Open on the before-and-after.** The Snapshot Report you ran to win the deal is your baseline. Put it beside today's Executive Report and the movement is the argument — no adjectives required.
- **Highlight what changed, not everything that exists.** Two improvements the owner can feel beats eight metrics they cannot.
- **Name what has not moved yet, before they do.** A gap you raise yourself is a plan. The same gap raised by the client is a complaint.
- **End on the next opportunity.** The report shows what is still unaddressed, which is the natural opening for the next package rather than a separate sales call.

## When a client says they never get it

- **Check the per-user notification toggle first.** It is the cause most of the time.
- **Then check spam.** Reports send through SendGrid and are commonly filtered by Microsoft 365 and Outlook.
- **"The page cannot be loaded"** is usually Microsoft Defender SafeLinks rewriting the link. Forwarding the email does not help. Either allowlist `sendgrid.com` in your Safe Links policy, or have the client view the report in Business App instead.
- **A PDF export missing its charts** is a browser print setting: enable **Background graphics** under the print dialog's options before saving.

:::tip Try it now
Open the oldest Executive Report on any client account and today's, side by side. Find one number that moved. That sentence is the most useful thing you will say in your next client meeting.
:::
