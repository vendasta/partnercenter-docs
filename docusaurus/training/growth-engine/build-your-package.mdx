## A package is an answer to a specific report

You left the last step with two gaps and a strength. That short list is the whole brief for this one. A package is not a catalogue you show every prospect; it is the fix for the report you just read aloud, and it should be small enough that the owner can repeat it back to you.

Northside Dental's report gave you the shape of it: patients love the clinic, and the directories disagree about its address. So the package has to fix the disagreement, keep the reviews flowing, and stop there. Two problems and a bit of maintenance is an easy yes. Nine products is a proposal that needs a committee.

## From gap to product

Work down your shortlist and put one product against each gap. The report helps here: each Snapshot section names the kind of solution it is pointing at, so you are matching a graded weakness to a thing you sell rather than guessing.

- **The listings gap** is an accuracy problem, and accuracy is what listing management is for. Northside Dental's address needs to be the same everywhere the search engines look.
- **The reviews strength** is worth protecting. A section the business aces is the easiest product conversation you will have, because you are selling continuity rather than repair.
- **Leave the rest out.** Anything you cannot fix, or the owner did not react to, does not belong in version one. You are not trying to bill every gap on the page.

Before you can put a product in a package at all, you have to be selling it. Products come from **Partner Center** → **Marketplace** → **Discover Products**, where each one shows its wholesale cost and a suggested retail price. **Start Selling** adds it to your store in one click.

:::info Only products you sell appear in the package builder
The package constructor's **+ Add item** list shows products you are currently selling and nothing else. If a product you expected is missing, that is why — go back to **Discover Products** and start selling it first.
:::

## Build the package

Packages live at **Partner Center** → **Marketplace** → **Packages**. There are two ways in, and the fast one is worth knowing about even if you rarely use it.

- **Start from a recommended bundle.** The **Recommended Packages** tab holds pre-built bundles. **Add to Store** takes one as-is; **Copy** on the Package Details page gives you an editable duplicate you can rename, re-price, and cut down. Copying is usually the better move, because a bundle built for everyone is rarely the answer to one report.
- **Start from scratch.** **Create Package** gives you an empty constructor. This is the honest choice when you are pricing against a specific Snapshot.

Either way you end up in the same builder, and the parts that matter are the item list on the left and the money panel on the right.

## Price it two ways

The builder lets you price from either end, and most partners use both in the same sitting.

**Per line item.** Click the price on any item and set it. Each item keeps its own billing frequency, so a monthly service and a one-time setup can sit in one package. The package total recalculates as you go.

**Across the whole package.** Click the **package total**, type the number you actually intend to charge, and the system distributes it back across the items using a weighted split based on each item's retail value and frequency. This is the one to reach for when you have already decided the package is a $499-a-month package and you are working backwards.

Setting the total first and then nudging individual items is a normal workflow, not a conflict.

**Setup fees** are one-time charges. Worth knowing where they land: a setup fee is allocated to a one-time item if the package has one, and to the first item if it does not.

**Read the money panel before you quote.** **Wholesale Cost** is what the components cost you. **Profit at Purchase** is retail minus wholesale. Quoting a number you have not checked against that panel is how a package ends up selling at a loss.

:::warning If Package Profit shows as unavailable
That is almost always a currency mismatch between the package and the market it sits in. Check **Marketplace** → **Manage Store** → **Currency** — one currency per market, and anything outside USD, CAD, and AUD needs a conversion rate you maintain yourself.
:::

## Decide how the client buys it

**Purchase behavior** has three settings, and it decides what the client can actually do when they see the package.

- **Add to Shopping Cart** — the client can buy it themselves. This is what you want for anything self-serve.
- **Contact Sales** — the package shows a button instead of a price. It needs a salesperson assigned through a contact form, or the button will not render at all.
- **External URL** — sends the client somewhere else entirely.

Two combinations catch people out. **Starting at** pricing and **Contact Sales** are both incompatible with shopping-cart checkout, so if you have set either one, do not expect a cart. And the checkout button in Business App needs two things switched on behind the scenes: Orders enabled for your account, and Vendasta Payments configured.

You also control how much of the package the client can see. The presets run from the package name alone, through contents visible with prices hidden, to contents and prices both visible. **Contents are hidden by default**, and the setting carries through to the store, Business App, and invoices.

## The trap worth knowing before it costs you a meeting

A product marked available in your store is still invisible to the client until it sits inside a **published** package assigned to a **visible** store category. Partners lose real time to this one: the product is switched on, the store looks empty, and nothing appears broken.

A few smaller ones in the same family:

- **You cannot add the same product twice** to a package. Use an add-on with multiple purchases allowed instead.
- **Bulk price updates across packages are not supported.** One package at a time.
- **Older packages migrate the first time you open and save them** in the constructor. The total is preserved, but the per-item breakdown will look new, and packages need this newer pricing model before they work with invoices or cart checkout.
- **Draft preview links work for anyone holding the link.** That is deliberate, for sending to a colleague — just do not treat it as private.

:::tip Try it now
Say your package out loud as one sentence: what it fixes, what it costs, and what the client gets each month. If it takes more than a breath, it has too many products in it.
:::
