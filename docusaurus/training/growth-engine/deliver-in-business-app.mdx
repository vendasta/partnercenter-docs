## The product is now theirs, under your name

The order activated, so the work exists. What it does not yet have is a person looking at it. This step is about handing over: getting the owner into Business App, under your branding, seeing the thing they just paid for.

Northside Dental's owner has never logged in. What they see in the first thirty seconds decides whether the next month is a partnership or an invoice they forgot the reason for.

Watch the tour your client gets, then we will do the partner-side setup that makes it appear.

## Add the client as a user

Nothing above happens until the owner has a login. From **Partner Center** → **Accounts** → **Manage Accounts**, open the account and use **Add users** → **Create User**. Enter their email and name, and note the **Send welcome email** checkbox — it is selected by default, and clearing it means the client gets a login they never hear about.

You can also work centrally from **Accounts** → **Manage Users** → **Create user** and assign the account there.

Email addresses have to be unique across the whole system, so a client who is already a user somewhere will throw an error rather than duplicate.

## What you can and cannot change about the welcome email

This catches people out, so it is worth being precise. The welcome email is only partly yours.

| Element | Yours to change? |
|---|---|
| Logo and brand colours | Yes, from your white-label settings |
| Sender name and email domain | Yes, from email domain settings |
| A short custom introductory message | Yes, added when you create the user |
| Subject line | No, hard-coded |
| Body content | No, hard-coded |

There is a branding wrinkle worth knowing if you run more than one market: the **initial** creation email always uses your **default** market branding regardless of which market the account belongs to. Resending from Account Details uses the account's market; resending from the Users page uses the default. If a client's first email looks like the wrong brand, that is why.

:::info If you want a real onboarding sequence
The welcome email is a login handoff, not onboarding. For something richer, build a campaign at **Marketing** → **Campaigns** → **Product Adoption**, then switch it on at **Customize Business App** → **Notifications** → **New customer experience** → **Onboarding campaign**. It fires on the client's first login only.
:::

Some partners are on an Early Access experience where the checkbox reads **Send Onboarding Email** instead, with a **Preview** button beside it. Where that is switched on it replaces the classic welcome email for that user rather than sending both. If you do not see it, you are on the classic flow described above.

## How activated products show up

Products you activate appear on the client's **My Products** page automatically, and anything you add later appears without you doing anything. Clients never see products they have not purchased.

The client controls their own view from there. Each product card has a **pin icon** in its top right, and pinning moves it to the top of the navigation and the products card. Two details worth passing on: pinning is **per user**, so two people at the same business can have different layouts, and the **first four pinned items appear on the dashboard**.

If you need to narrow what one person sees, use **Manage Users** → **Edit Permissions** and enable only the allowed products. The catch: a product has to be active on the account before it appears in Edit Permissions at all.

## The invitation you cannot send

When the owner wants to bring their office manager in, you cannot do it for them by logging in as them. Invitations have to come from someone signed directly into the account — either an admin user or an existing Business App user. Impersonating and clicking invite does not work.

The client's own route is **Invite team member** at the top of their Business App home page: first name, last name, email, **Send invite**.

Every user added or removed is logged in the CRM activity feed on both the company and the contact record, including who did it. Useful when someone asks how a person got access.
