## The close happens in the platform, not beside it

Everything so far has been preparation. This is the step where Northside Dental becomes a client: you turn the package into an order, the owner signs and pays on the same screen, and the products switch on by themselves.

That last part is the bit to understand properly before you send anything. When a client pays during approval, activation is automatic and **cannot be switched off**. That is usually exactly what you want — signature, payment, and delivery in one motion. It is also the single most common surprise on this workflow, so it is worth knowing before you find out live.

:::warning Proposal Builder is not the tool for this
If you learned this motion on Proposal Builder, that product is now legacy. The current path is **Commerce** → **Orders**, and the Orders workflow itself was redesigned in July 2025. Older walkthroughs referring to a "Sales Orders" flow will not match what you see.
:::

## Switch on the option before you need it

Sending an order for customer approval *with payment* is an admin setting, and it is off until someone turns it on. Do this once.

At **Partner Center** → **Administration** → **Order Settings**, find the **Salesperson submit actions** card and confirm both boxes:

- **Allow salespeople to send an order to customers for approval**
- **Orders sent for approval by salespeople can accept payment**

While you are there, set your **Customer terms of service** — that is the text the client agrees to on the approval screen, and it is worth writing once rather than discovering it is empty mid-deal.

## Build the order

Orders start at **Partner Center** → **Commerce** → **Orders** → **Create Order**. You can also start from the account itself, or from a company profile in the CRM, which is often faster because the account is already selected.

Then:

1. **Confirm the account.** Ordering against the wrong account cannot be undone by transfer — it has to be cancelled and re-created, so check this first.
2. **Add the package**, choosing editions where a product offers them.
3. **Review retail pricing and quantities.** Prices carry over from the package and can be overridden here without changing the stored package.
4. **Set billing terms** if the deal has a start date or a fixed term.

**Billing terms** are reachable two ways on any line item: click the blue **Automatically Renews** link under the product name, or use the item's kebab menu and choose **Edit Billing Terms**. The modal sets a start date and a number of renewals.

The fixed-renewal behaviour deserves reading twice: after the final renewal the product becomes inaccessible **and its associated data is removed**. Contract duration, by contrast, is internal record-keeping only and does not deactivate anything.

## Send it for approval, with payment

With the order built, choose **Send For Customer Approval**, indicate that payment is required, and pick the contact who should receive it. The order moves to **Awaiting customer approval**.

What the client gets is a **Contract Awaiting Approval** email. On the page it links to, they see your terms of service, sign to approve, and enter card details.

The moment the card is approved, all of this happens without you:

- The order is **activated**, or scheduled if you set a future start date
- **Subscriptions are created**
- An **invoice is generated** with **Paid** status
- Payment details land on the order's **PAYMENT** tab, and billing information becomes the account default if none was set
- Order status becomes **Activated**

If the card is declined, the order picks up a **Payment declined** tag and moves to **DECLINED**. You can convert it back to a draft and reprocess rather than starting over.

:::warning Automatic activation cannot be disabled
When a customer pays during approval, product activation triggers automatically and there is no setting to prevent it. If you need payment before delivery, you have two real options: set a **future contract start date** on the line items, or use a manual invoice flow and activate by hand.
:::

## The other route, and why the difference matters

Customer approval is not the only way to submit. If you **Submit** instead, the order goes to **Pending** for an admin with **Can Manage Orders** to approve — and here activation is **not** automatic.

An approved order still sits there until someone ticks the billing-agreement box (**I understand that I will be billed for activated products**) and chooses **Activate Immediately** or **Activate on Contract Start Date**.

That gap is a feature. Leaving the box unchecked is how you approve an order, invoice the client, collect payment, and only then switch the products on. It is the deliberate answer to the automatic-activation problem above.

Learners conflate these two routes constantly, so hold onto the difference: **customer approval with payment activates by itself; admin approval waits for a human.**

## When it does not go smoothly

- **Status shows Deferred.** The approval email failed to deliver. Check the address, then check SPF and DMARC on your sending domain.
- **The client got two emails.** Expected. Orders near their expiry date trigger an automatic reminder, usually a day or two before. Get the approval or cancel the order to stop them.
- **You need to resend.** Open the order and use **Resend to a customer**, then confirm the contact.
- **Something failed to activate.** A red badge appears on the **Fulfillment** card. Fix it from the item's kebab menu with **Edit and resubmit**, or dismiss, correct, and re-order.
- **You need to change something after sending.** Free editing only exists in draft. Salespeople cannot edit after submission at all; admins with the right permission can adjust a pending order before approval. Anything else means cancelling and recreating.
- **Cancelling is one-way.** Cancelled orders cannot be restored — use **Duplicate** instead. And cancelling an order does not cancel the underlying subscriptions.

:::info Activation emails to clients are off by default
Switching a product on does not tell the client. If you want that email, build it: **Automations** → **Create Automation**, trigger on the activation event, and add a **Send Email** action aimed at the client contact. This replaced the old automatic activation email.
:::
