## Date unknown — Lily Yip (Vendasta CS) & "Tech Master" / Tom, solo cybersecurity-consultancy partner (onboarding call, screen share)

**Source:** [CALL] Second onboarding call — testing and installing the chat receptionist widget on a Shopify site, plus AI Employee Setup/Optimization Plan products

Partner had already tested his voice receptionist on a prior call; this session walked through configuring and installing the chat receptionist (chatbot).

1. Both employees live under **AI → Workforce**: Chat Receptionist and Voice Receptionist tiles side by side. Click **Configure** on Chat Receptionist to open its settings — same layout as the voice receptionist (Role, Capabilities, Knowledge sources).
2. **Role/behavior boundary:** the partner was worried about the bot "solving people's problems for free" and becoming an unpaid help desk. CS's fix was to add explicit scope language directly in the role text — e.g., "you are [X], you do not solve technical support issues, you only answer questions about the company and capture leads / book a consultation."
3. **Capabilities:** partner already had "Capture leads" and "Book appointment" (30-minute slot) enabled. More can be added via the **+** button on the capability list (from a pre-built library), or built from scratch via the blue **custom capability** tab at the bottom left, which opens a blank form: Name, Description, and Prompt (the prompt is the literal instruction text the AI reads for that task).
4. **Knowledge sources** default to just two: the business website and the Google Business Profile. PDFs/Q&A docs can also be uploaded. CS was explicit this is a hard boundary — "the AI chatbot only has knowledge on [what you've fed it]... they can't share anything outside of that" — addressing the partner's prompt-injection/data-leakage concern (he runs a cybersecurity company). Suggested mitigation for injection-style probing: add a capability/role instruction telling the bot not to continue the conversation if a suspicious pattern is detected.
5. **Widget install steps (JS walkthrough):** inside the Chat Receptionist's own settings, tabs run across the top — Listing / Email / Message / **Settings**. Settings holds the widget appearance config (color/branding, set on an earlier call), and further down the same page is the actual embed-code section.
6. Two install snippets are offered: **"Website JavaScript"** and **"WordPress JavaScript."** For this partner's Shopify site, CS said to use the plain **Website JavaScript** option (not the WordPress-specific one) — copy it and paste it into Shopify's backend/theme code to install the widget.
7. **Package clarification that came up mid-call:** this partner's base subscription tier (Starter) includes only the chat receptionist, not voice. He'd separately purchased **Conversations AI Premium** specifically to test both chat and voice together (he mentioned he wouldn't want to pay "$600/month" to keep testing indefinitely, though CS didn't confirm that figure).
8. Once installed, all chat receptionist conversations land in the same **Conversations** tab/inbox as voice calls and SMS.
9. **CRM tie-in:** contacts captured by the chat receptionist (name + phone) auto-create CRM contacts, same as voice-receptionist captures, visible under Contacts with a "record source" field showing which channel captured them.
10. **Selling point CS reinforced:** the chat receptionist "screens calls" — filtering spam/cold-call noise before it interrupts the business owner's day. Called out explicitly as a strong pitch for home-services/trades clients.
11. **Marketplace products for setup/support** (partner asked about paying Vendasta to do this work for his future clients instead of doing it himself):
    - **AI Employee Setup** — one-time **$249** wholesale. Despite sounding per-employee, it actually covers both the chat and voice receptionist together ("two in one") for one client account; the fulfillment form asks which employee needs setup. Includes an onboarding call, branding, a live training call, and a 30-day check-in — after 30 days the client is on their own. Setup calls are white-label: the Vendasta rep represents the partner's agency on the call, not Vendasta.
    - **AI Optimization Plan** — ongoing-support counterpart, **$37/month wholesale, 12-month minimum**; covers continued prompt/automation edits after the initial 30 days (e.g., client wants a new capability or automation tweak later).

## Date unknown — Robert Kadar & Diane Osgerchian (agency partners) with Umair Khan (Vendasta CS)

**Source:** [CALL] Billing-bug report plus hands-on AI Employee build for two beauty-salon pilot clients (Davita Salon, Studio Salon)

1. **Billing bug reported at the top of the call:** partner downgraded/reduced a service and was unexpectedly charged an extra fee tied to a "third-party service." Umair's live assessment: "that kind of looks like a bug to me... it shouldn't automatically charge you for this existing service... that should be refunded back to you guys." Not resolved on the call — no root cause given, just flagged for a refund and backend investigation.
2. **A2P/SMS compliance gate:** before enabling SMS on a Conversations AI Premium trial, Umair ran an internal A2P compliance report against the client's website — checks for a published privacy policy, terms of service, and a separate **SMS terms** clause (called out as something partners commonly don't know exists as its own requirement). Davita Salon's site failed — no privacy policy or SMS terms — so the team's decision was to launch the one-month Conversations AI Premium trial (chat + voice) but hold off enabling SMS until those pages are published.
3. **Voice receptionist build (Davita Salon, Broadway location):**
   - Enable Conversations AI Premium → AI Workforce → middle tile "Voice Receptionist" → Configure.
   - Named it "Nikki" (matched to the chat receptionist's name for consistency) — the name is what it says when answering ("Hi, this is Nikki").
   - Client wanted calls picked up after ~5 rings during business hours.
   - **Mobile call forwarding:** expand "Mobile" in Settings to get the activation codes — dial `*61*` + the AI's phone number + `**` + a ring-delay-in-seconds value + `#` from the client's own phone to activate. The team read the codes aloud on a stopwatch to judge how many rings ~10 seconds equals (settled on 10 seconds ≈ 4-5 rings). This only works for mobile — the client must personally dial the code from their phone; Vendasta's side only configures the setting.
   - **Landline call forwarding (this client's actual line):** the in-app codes only cover "forward all calls immediately" (`*72`) or "forward when busy" — there's no seconds-delay option built into the landline codes. Fix: the client must contact their own landline provider and ask them to set up conditional forwarding to the AI's number after a specific number of seconds/rings — that part is carrier-managed, not configurable inside Vendasta. Script the team sent the client: "Here is the AI voice receptionist phone number. Please contact your phone provider and ask them to forward inbound calls to this number after the phone rings five or six times, or after ~10 seconds." Confirmed this works the same whether the client's line is landline or VoIP.
   - **Advanced Voice Options:** Semantic VAD (AI judges natural pause vs. simple silence detection) left on semantic; Response Eagerness (low/medium/high, controls how fast the AI jumps in) set to medium to start, with guidance to drop to low if callers report being cut off.
   - **Voice model selection:** tested live via the "share tab audio" screen-share toggle. CS's positioning: **Deepgram (Aura)** is strongest for English/Spanish specifically; **OpenAI/ChatGPT realtime** is the most broadly multilingual; **ElevenLabs** sounds the most human. Client picked a specific OpenAI feminine voice option as the best fit for a hair salon.
4. **Booking limitation discovered live:** the "Book appointments" capability requires connecting a calendar via **CRM → My Meetings** in Business App — it does **not** talk to the salon's existing third-party booking system on their live site (Go Booker, named explicitly). No native two-way sync exists between Go Booker and Vendasta booking; Umair floated a possible webhook/API bridge but had no confirmed answer.
5. **Workaround decided on the call:** instead of reconciling two calendars, configure the AI to collect the caller's preferred service/day/time/location/stylist and tell them a team member will follow up to confirm — not attempt to auto-book. This replaced the default **"Record preferred booking time"** capability, which the team found doesn't actually book anything (just logs a stated preference) and "reads as a broken booking flow" to a caller.
6. **Custom capability build workflow (demonstrated end to end):**
   - Started from the out-of-box "Record preferred booking time" template, then Add Capability.
   - Drafted the new questions live (service, day/time, which of the two locations — Broadway/Union Square vs. Second Avenue/Upper East Side —, stylist preference).
   - Pasted the existing capability prompt plus the new requirements into ChatGPT (their tool of choice; Claude/Gemini/Copilot work the same way), asked it to "combine them into one single markdown code block," then pasted the result back into the capability's prompt field, replacing the old prompt entirely.
   - Saved and called the AI number live to test; iterated once (dropped the stylist-preference question when it felt like it lengthened the call too much), then re-tested by phone until the flow correctly asked for service/day/time/location and said a team member would confirm rather than falsely confirming a booking.
7. **Website-knowledge training bug hit live (unresolved):** the chatbot kept showing "trained on 1 of 1 pages" even after switching the knowledge-source mode from **Sitemap** (which had only picked up 3 links) to **Follow links** (which should crawl up to ~100 pages). As a result the bot didn't know the salon had a second location and incorrectly told a test caller "we don't have an Upper East Side location," even though it's on the site. Flagged to escalate to Umair; not fixed by end of call.
8. **Workaround for the training gap:** paste the relevant page text directly into the Knowledge Base as a custom "Uploaded Document" instead of relying on the URL crawler.
9. **Widget install/hand-off:** since the agency doesn't host the client's site, the plan was to copy the JavaScript embed code from the Chat Receptionist's Settings (same section holds floating-vs-banner widget style plus primary/accent/text color pickers) and send it to the client's outside web developer ("Submit Me") to install.
10. **Pricing decided for future clients (post-pilot):** normal pricing is a **$250 one-time setup fee plus $150/month**; for these first two pilot salons both fees were waived to prove it out, but going forward the plan is to offer new prospects "$250 setup fee, first month of the $150 service fee free" rather than waiving everything.

## Date unknown — Ehab Tarabay, Anderson Business Advisors (with Jeff Leach & Braedon Gaudet, Vendasta)

**Source:** [CALL] POC/demo prep — Conversations AI SMS employee ("Andy") built for a webinar-registration speed-to-lead flow, ahead of a Barcelona conference demo

1. **Use case, phased:** Phase 1 (the one demoed) — a lead registers for a webinar/event, lands in Salesforce, and **Andy** (a custom Conversations AI SMS employee) immediately texts to ask what the person hopes to learn from the webinar, to drive engagement and lift attendance. Phase 2 (built but not the demo focus) — any web-form lead more broadly gets qualified by AI and, if it meets criteria, offered a meeting with an advisor; if not ready, offered webinar registration instead.
2. Sandbox build: a form-fill on a demo site (built separately via Vendasta Vibe — not itself Conversations AI) triggers an automation that fires Andy's first SMS.
3. **Live test on the call:** filled the demo form with a real phone number → received an SMS from Andy → replied with a specific interest ("trying to learn more about asset protection") → Andy replied with tailored info drawn from its trained knowledge.
4. **Human takeover:** a rep can take over a live AI conversation any time by replying directly from the backend Conversations view. If the human doesn't reply, Andy resumes automatically after a **1-hour timeout**, with full prior context intact. AI replies can also be switched off entirely for a given conversation to run it fully manually.
5. **Access/notifications:** team members got portal access via a "Welcome to Business App" email; by default every new lead/message triggers an email notification, which Vendasta turned off after the demo to avoid spamming the team ("hey, your AI receptionist has captured a new lead").
6. **Employee configuration pattern:** Role = overall objective/behavior; Generative AI model is selectable (Vendasta stays model-agnostic, not locked to one LLM); Capabilities = discrete tasks (e.g., access CRM, book into the next webinar or with an advisor — demo used placeholder data since real Salesforce wasn't wired up yet); Knowledge = trained on 100 pages of Anderson's actual website plus manually-added placeholder webinar-schedule data.
7. **Guardrails:** none were configured in this sandbox on purpose, so the team could probe it during rehearsal. Braedon demonstrated adding a guardrail directly into the Role text (an instruction that any off-topic question gets a canned "Nice try, that's not part of this demo" reply) — and showed the edit takes effect immediately on the same live conversation being tested by phone, no republish step needed.
8. **Channel coverage confirmed:** SMS, web chat (same underlying AI agent as SMS — a lead who chats on the site instead of texting still lands in the same CRM record), email, Facebook Messenger, Instagram Messenger, and WhatsApp — six channels total. WhatsApp called out as very important outside North America (Anderson is acquiring a Dubai-based business whose lead-gen runs on ~50 WhatsApp numbers).
9. **International SMS tested live:** a US/Canada number works out of the box via Twilio; a Netherlands mobile number was also texted live and it did arrive, though Vendasta flagged that other European markets often use non-Twilio carriers, so delivery there should be tested per-country rather than assumed.
10. **Lead-source labeling:** the CRM distinguishes and displays how each contact arrived — "direct SMS," "form fill," or "web chat conversation" — as separate record-source tags on the same Contacts/Companies view.
11. **Automations behind the demo:** (a) event-registration-form-submission automation fires the initial SMS, editable in the Automations panel if the wording needs to change; (b) a second automation emails a staff notification whenever someone has a web-chat conversation with the AI receptionist. Automations can also be fired manually against a saved contact list via a "Start automation" button.
12. **Scope note:** this build used zero real system integrations (fake CRM/webinar data, no live Salesforce connection) so it could be spun up fast without the Solution Architecture team; real Salesforce integration for production "takes a little bit more" time per Braedon.

*(Note: the demo website itself was built with Vendasta Vibe from a single prompt — that content belongs to the `vibe` topic, not filed here; see flag in the report below.)*

## Date unknown — Alistair George (Vendasta) with Sandy Prager, John Werneke, Chris Deianni, Danieal Barratt, Sean Schroeder (Neighborly / Air Serve of Clearwater)

**Source:** [CALL] Live testing session for the webchat/lead-flow automation ("LFA") feeding Air Serve of Clearwater's ServiceTitan POS

1. **Setup:** Air Serve of Clearwater (a Neighborly-family brand) is integrated with **ServiceTitan** via a two-way contact sync. The team tested four scenarios back to back: (1) an existing contact — synced in from ServiceTitan originally via "external API" record source or the original October data push — resubmitting the website contact form; (2) a brand-new contact filling the form; (3) a contact with a stale "booked" status in the CRM refilling the form; (4) a web-chat submission (different, faster path).
2. **Timing/flow, confirmed step by step:**
   - The website itself holds a form submission for **~2.5 minutes** before forwarding it to Vendasta — deliberately, to give the person a window to just book directly on the web form instead.
   - Once received, the automation sends the first SMS ("SMS1"). Total elapsed time before first contact is faster for a **short form** (address/email/phone only) than a long form with upsells.
   - After SMS1 sends, the system waits **10 minutes** for a reply before pushing the lead to ServiceTitan — this delay exists specifically "to wait for a reply... because what we don't want to do is push twice."
   - If the person replies within that 10-minute window, the lead still pushes to ServiceTitan, but via the "chat summary" push (carries a conversation summary). If they don't reply, it pushes as a "dumb" lead push (a "lead only" flag, no conversation summary attached).
   - **Web-chat submissions skip most of this wait** — arrived in ~5 minutes total in the live test — because, per Alistair, "there's nothing to wait for" from the POS/website side; it's fully self-contained.
   - Total lead visibility lag for a standard form fill can run **10-15 minutes** before it shows up in ServiceTitan for the franchise owner to see.
3. **Franchisee pushback surfaced live:** John raised that 10-15 minutes feels long to an owner used to instant callbacks — a prior location ("Sarah") had LFA turned off entirely because she was reaching customers faster manually than the automation could. Team's working answer (not a fix, a reframe): ~80% of this business's volume is inbound emergency phone calls handled the normal way; the web form only covers the remaining ~20%, and most of those people will call anyway if urgent — so the automation is really only "competing" with itself for the minority of people willing to wait.
4. **Known duplicate-lead bug:** if a contact replies right around the edge of the 10-minute window, both the reply-triggered push and the delayed "dumb" push can fire, creating two separate lead records in ServiceTitan for the same person — a known issue that also shows up as inflated lead-count reporting ("it's doubling... showing two versus one").
5. **Stale-status bug found live:** resubmitting the form for a contact with a leftover/stale "booked" status (never reclassified by the franchisee, common at locations juggling 150+ old bookings) caused the cloud function to return "no point of sale push required" and skip pushing to ServiceTitan entirely. Team agreed this is wrong — a stale POS status should not suppress nurturing on a brand-new inbound inquiry; correct behavior should be to still push to POS and still send the owner-notification email either way. Flagged for engineering root-cause and fix, not resolved on the call.
6. **Scale context given:** the stale-status bug affects "way less than 1%" of leads. The bigger problem the whole LFA testing effort was meant to fix — leads synced in via the original external-API/POS push that were never being nurtured at all — was confirmed solved by this point in testing.
7. **Test method used deliberately:** the team used a real production contact's actual info (with advance notice given to the franchise owner) rather than fake data, specifically to mirror real automation behavior exactly, relying on the 2.5-minute website hold as an abort window if anything misbehaved.
8. **Two small but real gotchas surfaced:** (a) the AI's SMS landed in the recipient's "Unknown Senders" filtered folder on iPhone — a phone-side setting, not a Vendasta issue, but worth mentioning during client onboarding; (b) feature request raised that the SMS sender should ideally display a branded name ("Air Serve") instead of a raw phone number.

## Date unknown — Braedon Gaudet, Jeff Leach, Cody Baier (Vendasta) with Amy Volatile, Emily Grotkin, Brian Heppner (Dynamico, a HubSpot implementation/RevOps partner)

**Source:** [CALL] Platform-overview/demo call exploring a HubSpot-integration or referral relationship — Conversations AI portion only (ecosystem overview, Vibe, and other-product content filed/flagged separately)

1. **Positioning:** Conversations AI is framed as the flagship "speed to lead" tool inside Vendasta's broader "AI-optimized flywheel" (attract/convert/engage) — it handles missed-call text-backs, SMS, phone calls, and website chat from one agent.
2. **Four reasons partners buy it, per Braedon:** (1) losing leads / can't keep up with volume, (2) consolidating a fragmented vendor stack, (3) reporting visibility, (4) efficiency/time savings for franchisees.
3. **ROI/sizing question directly asked by Amy** (how many leads/month justify the cost): Braedon's answer — no hard minimum; roughly a **30% increase in captured leads** is typical once Conversations AI is introduced. Contrast raised by Amy from her own past experience: a competitor AI-calling tool ("Lumen") needed ~50 leads/month to pencil out because of its pricing; Jeff's framing was that value is highest, almost automatic, for businesses that don't answer phones during business hours (home services, salons) and that Vendasta's pricing is "a lot less than Lumen."
4. **Named case study — Neighborly:** Molly Maid's use of Conversations AI drove a **372% increase in revenue** over Q1 2024, tied to leads that were previously being missed (~30% of their leads were going uncaptured before). Neighborly named Vendasta "vendor of the year" three years running (a competitor broke the streak in year four, then Vendasta won it back the year after).
5. **Multi-location dashboard demo:** for a large brand, conversations volume ran roughly **5 per minute**; the dashboard tracks lead source (form fill, missed-call text-back, or website chat conversation) and shows real booked-appointment examples, viewable at both the single-location and corporate/multi-location level.
6. **Franchise-development (FranDev) use case — Authority Brands (e.g., Mosquito Squad):** Conversations AI is deliberately tuned to act as a **filter, not a lead-creation tool**, for franchise-candidate inquiries — the goal is separating serious candidates from noise before handing off to the franchise sales team, since "you're not gonna sell a franchise on a website, but you are going to separate the noise from the real candidates."
7. **Custom internal-ops agent pattern:** some franchise systems build a non-customer-facing "operations manager" agent trained on the internal operations manual, so franchisees can ask procedural questions (e.g., "how do I lock up?") instead of calling corporate or digging through the manual.
8. **HubSpot integration:** native two-way sync for contacts, companies, and notes; anything outside that direct sync can be bridged via Zapier, webhooks, or APIs. Conversation summaries can push automatically into the corresponding HubSpot contact record. One existing partner integration was shown routing captured leads through an **email parser directly into Client Tether** as an alternative to a native connector, because that's the route the client specifically wanted.
9. **Widget/install mechanics:** a single JavaScript snippet can go on any website and follows visitors around the page; WordPress has its own dedicated plug-in version of the same widget.
10. **Configuration pattern (same shown to the Anderson demo):** connect accounts, set Role, set Capabilities, add Knowledge sources — Dynamico's team noted this looked "fairly familiar" versus HubSpot's own AI agents; Vendasta's differentiation pitch was being more model-agnostic, since HubSpot's native AI agents are built directly on top of the HubSpot Smart CRM and are less flexible about swapping models.
11. **Scaling mechanism for multi-location brands:** build and validate the Conversations AI configuration on one location first, then use **Admin Center → Accounts and account templates** to pick which parts of that setup (e.g., the web-chat configuration) to copy across every other location. Each duplicated instance still trains itself on that specific location's own website/knowledge base rather than inheriting the source location's knowledge wholesale.
