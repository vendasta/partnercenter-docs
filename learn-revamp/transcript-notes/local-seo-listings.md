## Date unknown — Certified Partner Roundtable (facilitated by Jehan Zouak; partners present: Brett Prieskorn, Simon Papadopoulos, Joshua Iverson, Klint Rudolph, Patrick Howell, Chris Geer) — peer discussion on the AI Search agent / SEO Pro tooling

**Source:** [CALL] `certified-partner-roundtable-seo-ai-workforce-crm.txt` — a roughly 15-minute segment of a longer certified-partner roundtable focused on the "AI Search" employee/SEO Pro app, its underlying data source, and where an SEO-focused agent should surface to clients.

1. Product identity: partners use "AI search agent," "AI search employee," and "AI search expert" interchangeably. It lives in the AI Workforce area and is tied to the **SEO Pro** app / organic-ranking data — distinct from the local-listings side of Local SEO.
2. What it actually does today, per a partner (Patrick Howell) who tested it: "It doesn't actually do any work. It's just... theories, strategy" — i.e., it produces written SEO advice and keyword/content recommendations but doesn't execute changes itself. Multiple partners (Patrick, Simon Papadopoulos) already do equivalent strategy work manually or via Claude plus external tools (Search Atlas, ahrefs), and were unclear on the incremental value of also paying for the Vendasta version.
3. Real value case, per Brett Prieskorn: Vendasta pays for a seat of **"data for SEO"** — described on the call as "the newest open API SEO platform" for collecting and pulling SEO data — and wires it into this agent. Buying the Vendasta agent gets a partner (or a client's internal team) that same underlying keyword/ranking data at a flat, predictable cost instead of licensing their own data-for-SEO API seat and paying per-query credits directly.
4. Roadmap gap acknowledged live: the SEO agent is meant to eventually connect to the **Blogger** agent so SEO research can directly drive content production — that integration does not exist yet. Brett's suggestion to the group/Vendasta: stop treating Blogger and the SEO agent as two separate purchases/toggles; combine them into one "SEO content agent."
5. Client-visibility control gap (raised in this context, not SEO-specific but relevant to how an SEO-focused agency would deploy this): there's no way today to toggle individual AI Workforce agents on/off for client visibility. Example from Klint Rudolph: wants clients to see the voice and chat receptionists but not the Blogger or SEO agent — today it's all-or-nothing at the workforce level. Partners compared this to the long-standing complaint that CRM fields also can't be hidden per-client.
6. Business risk if a client gets direct access to the SEO agent's output (raised by Simon Papadopoulos and Brett Prieskorn): SEO work covers roughly "100 things," and an agency typically only bills for a handful per month. If a client runs the AI Search agent themselves and sees the other ~95 unaddressed opportunities, they may start dictating priorities or feel misled ("why aren't you already doing this"). Concrete field example from Chris Geer: a two-location childcare-center client emailed SEO keyword suggestions she'd sourced from ChatGPT; Chris's response was to screenshot the client's *own* named/customized AI Search employee already living inside her Business App and remind her it does the same work — a deliberate move to keep her engaging inside the platform rather than external AI tools, and to reframe the moment as an upsell opening (the client's curiosity becomes a reason to buy dedicated SEO service, not just the existing blog/social retainer).
7. Related defensibility concern (Brett Prieskorn): if a client uses the agency's own AI Search agent and it gives bad or incomplete guidance, the agency has a much weaker defense than when a client gets bad info from Google or an outside tool on their own — "your device and tool is what told me the wrong information" is harder to answer than "here's how SEO actually works; your outside prompting doesn't have the data we have."
8. Concrete platform-improvement idea proposed on the call: make the AI Search agent selectable as one of the "expert" chat agents embeddable in a **Snapshot Report** (the bottom-right chat widget), alongside or instead of the chat receptionist — several partners called the generic chat receptionist "the least informed agent to be there" for SEO-specific conversations. Ideally a partner could choose which of their AI employees appears there based on their own specialty (SEO agency → SEO agent, social agency → social agent).
9. **Gotcha confirmed independently by two partners:** the chat agent embedded in a Snapshot Report is pulled from whichever AI employee is configured as the **"AI chat receptionist" in Partner Center** — not from the partner's own Business App instance. Brett discovered his Business App and Partner Center chat-receptionist configurations had drifted apart, and the live Snapshot Reports were serving the (wrong/outdated) Partner Center version. Simon reported the identical issue and resolved it by disabling his own Business Center instance and consolidating everything on the Partner Center configuration. Fix/checklist: if a Snapshot Report's embedded chat is saying something unexpected, check the **Partner Center** chat-receptionist config specifically, not the Business App one.
10. Maturity-curve framing offered by Jehan Zouak (Vendasta) for why the SEO agent — and AI employees generally — currently only advise rather than act: "the progression... has kind of been like, at first they are kind of more just... informational and then more capabilities get added and they become agentic." Informational-only is presented as an expected early phase, not a permanent limitation.

Out of scope for this note (belongs to other topics — see report-back): the same roundtable spends most of its time on API access frustrations, custom AI Workforce agents partners have built externally, social-AI adoption, and CRM/task-manager syncing with Google Calendar.

## Date unknown — Vendasta managed-services leadership team (vendor evaluation call)

**Source:** [CALL] Vendor demo — Vendasta's internal managed-services/AI-services leadership team evaluating a white-label AI-agent SEO platform (a former digital marketing agency turned SaaS vendor) as a possible fulfillment tool/marketplace partner, local SEO and Google Business Profile portion

The vendor's platform includes a dedicated Google Business Profile (GBP) management module that is relevant background for how a competing tool approaches local listings automation:
- On connecting a GBP account, the tool ingests everything currently live on the profile, then an AI model scores the existing profile and surfaces specific suggested improvements (e.g., "do you offer these services," "add more images"). The user accepts/rejects suggestions, then hits "push" to publish to the live GBP.
- After publishing, the tool can **lock** the GBP so no other user/admin can silently undo the changes — framed as preserving the integrity of completed optimization work.
- Built-in guardrail: the tool explicitly flags fields that risk triggering Google's re-verification process (business name change is the flagged example) and warns/double-confirms with the user before allowing that kind of edit, rather than letting it happen accidentally.
- A separate "citation score" audit checks a shorter list of major citation sources than Vendasta natively covers (the vendor rep candidly admitted their citation coverage was weaker than Vendasta's and that a partner comparing head-to-head likely wouldn't switch for citations specifically, only if bundled with the rest of the platform). It flags missing/inaccurate listings and lets you buy additional citations.
- A **local heat map** tool: import a business from the connected GBP or search by name/place ID, then place/move a map pin, adjust grid density and spacing, set a crawl/refresh interval and time-of-day, and get AI-suggested keywords to track. Running the report shows current map-pack ranking position at each grid point for a target keyword, with a timeline view of how rankings change over successive crawls. Positioned as a strong **sales tool**: side-by-side visualizations (e.g., green pins for your listing vs. red for a competitor) make it easy to show a prospect why they should switch.
- Separate standalone GBP auditing tool (usable even for non-customers, just by adding a business manually) runs a full audit covering NAP consistency, profile completeness, and competitor comparison on reviews/post frequency — output is shareable with the prospect/client as a report.
- The vendor also has an industry-benchmarking visualization showing which local ranking signals (reviews volume, citations, posting frequency, etc.) matter most by industry vertical, plus a "what if I scale up X" forecasting tool — again framed as more of a sales/education tool than an action tool.

Gotcha called out by the Vendasta team during the demo: they specifically asked about triggering re-verification and were satisfied that the vendor's tool proactively blocks/warns on risky field changes rather than just documenting the risk after the fact — something worth benchmarking Vendasta's own GBP tooling against.

## Date unknown — a Saskatchewan-based digital marketing agency owner with a Vendasta account manager (product walkthrough / pricing call)

**Source:** [CALL] Partner reviews Local SEO Pro pricing tiers, the Yext dependency, and a new Citation Builder add-on

1. **Local SEO Pro is tiered by account count (wholesale, per account):** **$13/account for 1–20 accounts**, dropping to **$11/account for 21+ accounts**.
2. **Yext is effectively a required companion product, not optional** — the account manager confirmed you can't fully populate directory listings without it. Cited cost ~**$35/account** wholesale.
3. **New add-on: Citation Builder**, found via **Marketplace → Discover Products → search "Citation Builder" → Start selling this add-on**. Priced at **~$5 USD ($5.50 CAD) per account**.
4. **What Citation Builder actually solves — data-aggregator refresh cycles:** smaller/local directories periodically (roughly every 6–8 months) pull refreshed business info from a small number of **large data aggregators**. If a partner has manually corrected a listing's info directly on a specific directory, but the underlying data aggregator still has old/incorrect info, the directory's periodic refresh from that aggregator can **silently overwrite the manually-corrected data back to the stale version**. Citation Builder is what keeps the data aggregators themselves in sync so this doesn't happen — a "one source of truth" listing update tool alone does not protect against this if the aggregator layer isn't also updated.
5. **Gotcha this creates for existing clients:** this issue applies retroactively to clients already running Local SEO Pro without Citation Builder.
6. **Email campaign deliverability tied to DNS/domain settings, but not fully resolved on the call:** a separate campaigns product's outbound emails were reportedly landing in recipients' junk/spam folders for the partner's clients despite DNS records already being configured; the account manager insisted correctly-configured DNS should prevent this — flagged as something the partner needs to re-verify rather than a known product bug.

## Date unknown — a physical-therapy franchise partner (co-owners) with a Vendasta sales rep (SEO/reputation/AI-chatbot proposal call)

**Source:** [CALL] Local SEO/listings diagnostic walkthrough for a seven-location physical-therapy clinic group

1. **Diagnostic framing used with the client:** rep built a location-by-location scorecard checking (a) keyword ranking for the core search term, (b) directory/listing accuracy, and (c) review volume, presented as "out of eight searches, if you're in five of them, they're finding someone else first."
2. **Stale-address listing example:** one clinic location's directory listings still pointed to a five-year-old former address — even though the location still ranks #1 organically for its core keyword in its current town, the rep flagged that inconsistent old-address data is exactly the kind of signal that confuses both traditional search engines and **AI/AEO ("answer engine optimization")/GEO ("generative engine optimization")** systems, which cross-reference information across the internet for accuracy before deciding who to recommend — even when it isn't currently hurting rankings, it's a risk to clean up.
3. **Keyword self-competition between a corporate/parent listing and an individual location listing:** when both a franchise's corporate office and one of its outpatient locations rank for the same keyword, they're not being penalized for existing twice — the fix is differentiating the **Google Business Profile descriptions/keywords** for each so corporate ranks for general/administrative queries while the specific location ranks for the service-seeking query.
4. **Practical tip for checking real rankings:** always check search rank in an **incognito browser tab** — a logged-in/cached browser session will show a personalized result set that doesn't reflect what a new searcher actually sees.
5. **Reviews-as-backlinks framing:** the rep explained that responding to Google/other reviews with strong, relevant keywords functions similarly to a backlink/directory reference in the eyes of both Google's ranking algorithm and AI systems — an active, keyword-rich review-response habit builds "authority figure" signal over time, distinct from just collecting more reviews.
6. **Live-math misstep as a process lesson:** the rep presented a single blended "average reviews per location" number without first showing the underlying per-location breakdown, which visibly confused the client mid-meeting until the rep walked back through the math live. Lesson for future proposal decks: show the per-location numbers alongside any aggregate/average figure, not the average alone.

## Date unknown — Alisha K, Rebekah King (Vendasta local listings team), Ashir Fazal, Czarina Sacdalan with Advance Local (Gina, Ally Wyle, and team) — white-label local listings management workflow: onboarding, GBP/Bing/Apple scope, and white-label communication
**Source:** [CALL] `local-listings-management-workflow-advance-local.txt` — a process-alignment call between Vendasta's Local Listings Management fulfillment team and a partner (Advance Local) transitioning full listings-management responsibility to Vendasta after losing the staff member who used to handle it in-house.

1. **Service scope, stated explicitly:** the **Local Listings Management** service covers exactly three platforms — **Google Business Profile, Bing, and Apple** (Maps/Business Connect) — not Facebook, Instagram, or Twitter, which the partner confirmed they don't need covered here anyway.
2. **Per-location workflow:** check for an existing GBP listing first; if none exists, create one from the submitted NAP details; if verification is needed, Vendasta works directly with Google (or with the partner) to complete it.
3. **Source-of-truth practice:** the intake/order **form** (not ad-hoc emails) is treated as the single source of truth for location details and for whether Vendasta should contact the end client directly — a dedicated form field toggles client-communication permission per account.
4. **White-label communication convention:** when Vendasta emails the end client directly (with authorization), it identifies itself as **"[marketing services] on behalf of [Partner Name]"** rather than as Vendasta (or any other Vendasta-owned brand name) — a live bug surfaced on this call where an invite email displayed a different Vendasta-owned brand name instead of the expected white-label name, confusing the partner into thinking a client had done something themselves; flagged to fix.
5. **Consolidated-email best practice:** partner explicitly asked Vendasta to batch listing updates/requests **by business** into a single email rather than many near-duplicate emails, since duplicates get lost — matching a practice the partner already followed internally.
6. **Booking-link handoff pattern:** for anything requiring a live verification step (e.g., a phone-code GBP verification, which has to happen in real time), Vendasta offered to embed its own scheduling link directly in emails to the end client so the partner can just forward it and let Vendasta run that specific call, while the partner keeps the relationship touchpoint.
7. **Apple Business Connect — the trickiest of the three platforms:** Apple often auto-generates a business listing by scraping public data/popularity signals with **no way to tell from the outside** whether a listing is self-claimed or auto-generated — Apple has no equivalent to Google's "who manages this" display. The only reliable way to know in advance is if the partner explicitly flags in the intake form that the client does **not** yet have an Apple listing.
