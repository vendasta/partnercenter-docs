## Date unknown — Certified Partner Roundtable (facilitated by Jehan Zouak; partners present: Brett Prieskorn, Simon Papadopoulos, Joshua Iverson, Klint Rudolph, Patrick Howell, Chris Geer) — peer discussion on the AI Search agent / SEO Pro tooling

**Source:** [CALL] `certified-partner-roundtable-seo-ai-workforce-crm.txt` — a roughly 15-minute segment of a longer certified-partner roundtable focused on the "AI Search" employee/SEO Pro app, its underlying data source, and where an SEO-focused agent should surface to clients.

1. Product identity: partners use "AI search agent," "AI search employee," and "AI search expert" interchangeably. It lives in the AI Workforce area and is tied to the **SEO Pro** app / organic-ranking data — distinct from the local-listings side of Local SEO.
2. What it actually does today, per a partner (Patrick Howell) who tested it: "It doesn't actually do any work. It's just... theories, strategy" — i.e., it produces written SEO advice and keyword/content recommendations but doesn't execute changes itself. Multiple partners (Patrick, Simon Papadopoulos) already do equivalent strategy work manually or via Claude plus external tools (Search Atlas, ahrefs), and were unclear on the incremental value of also paying for the Vendasta version.
3. Real value case, per Brett Prieskorn: Vendasta pays for a seat of **"data for SEO"** — described on the call as "the newest open API SEO platform" for collecting and pulling SEO data — and wires it into this agent. Buying the Vendasta agent gets a partner (or a client's internal team) that same underlying keyword/ranking data at a flat, predictable cost instead of licensing their own data-for-SEO API seat and paying per-query credits directly.
4. Roadmap gap acknowledged live: the SEO agent is meant to eventually connect to the **Blogger** agent so SEO research can directly drive content production — that integration does not exist yet. Brett's suggestion to the group/Vendasta: stop treating Blogger and the SEO agent as two separate purchases/toggles; combine them into one "SEO content agent."
5. Client-visibility control gap (raised in this context, not SEO-specific but relevant to how an SEO-focused agency would deploy this): there's no way today to toggle individual AI Workforce agents on/off for client visibility. Example from Klint Rudolph: wants clients to see the voice and chat receptionists but not the Blogger or SEO agent — today it's all-or-nothing at the workforce level. Partners compared this to the long-standing complaint that CRM fields also can't be hidden per-client.
6. Business risk if a client gets direct access to the SEO agent's output (raised by Simon Papadopoulos and Brett Prieskorn): SEO work covers roughly "100 things," and an agency typically only bills for a handful per month. If a client runs the AI Search agent themselves and sees the other ~95 unaddressed opportunities, they may start dictating priorities or feel misled ("why aren't you already doing this"). Concrete field example from Chris Geer: a two-location childcare-center client emailed SEO keyword suggestions she'd sourced from ChatGPT; Chris's response was to screenshot the client's *own* named/customized AI Search employee already living inside her Business App and remind her it does the same work — a deliberate move to keep her engaging inside the platform rather than external AI tools, and to reframe the moment as an upsell opening (the client's curiosity becomes a reason to buy dedicated SEO service, not just the existing blog/social retainer).
7. Related defensibility concern (Brett Prieskorn): if a client uses the agency's own AI Search agent and it gives bad or incomplete guidance, the agency has a much weaker defense than when a client gets bad info from Google or an outside tool on their own — "your device and tool is what told me the wrong information" is harder to answer than "here's how SEO actually works; your outside prompting doesn't have the data we have."
8. Concrete platform-improvement idea proposed on the call: make the AI Search agent selectable as one of the "expert" chat agents embeddable in a **Snapshot Report** (the bottom-right chat widget), alongside or instead of the chat receptionist — several partners called the generic chat receptionist "the least informed agent to be there" for SEO-specific conversations. Ideally a partner could choose which of their AI employees appears there based on their own specialty (SEO agency → SEO agent, social agency → social agent).
9. **Gotcha confirmed independently by two partners:** the chat agent embedded in a Snapshot Report is pulled from whichever AI employee is configured as the **"AI chat receptionist" in Partner Center** — not from the partner's own Business App instance. Brett discovered his Business App and Partner Center chat-receptionist configurations had drifted apart, and the live Snapshot Reports were serving the (wrong/outdated) Partner Center version. Simon reported the identical issue and resolved it by disabling his own Business Center instance and consolidating everything on the Partner Center configuration. Fix/checklist: if a Snapshot Report's embedded chat is saying something unexpected, check the **Partner Center** chat-receptionist config specifically, not the Business App one.
10. Maturity-curve framing offered by Jehan Zouak (Vendasta) for why the SEO agent — and AI employees generally — currently only advise rather than act: "the progression... has kind of been like, at first they are kind of more just... informational and then more capabilities get added and they become agentic." Informational-only is presented as an expected early phase, not a permanent limitation.

Out of scope for this note (belongs to other topics — see report-back): the same roundtable spends most of its time on API access frustrations, custom AI Workforce agents partners have built externally, social-AI adoption, and CRM/task-manager syncing with Google Calendar.

## Date unknown — Desiree Kupietz (Vendasta, partner/channel side) with Brandon Kuse and Ashley Colson (Uberall — Vendasta's listings/citation data partner) — Uberall listings integration, doctor.com add-on, and Local Pages review

**Source:** [CALL] `custom-reporting-bigquery-listings-data-request.txt` — vendor check-in between Vendasta and Uberall; the listings-specific portion of a longer call that also covers a BigQuery data-export request handled separately under snapshot-executive-reports.

1. There's an existing custom **"premium report"** (built for at least one partner) that surfaces listings data whenever that partner runs a Snapshot Report on an account. It currently only covers listings — Desiree separately asked the internal team (Alistair) to extend it to also show reputation and social data, and got confirmation that's doable, so the same report is being broadened beyond pure listings.
2. Listings fields discussed for that report/export, and which were kept vs. cut:
   - **Keep:** number of listing sources available, number of sources actually found/matched, Google Business Profile claimed status, and — flagged by Desiree as a gap — **reviews**, which weren't in the report yet and should be added.
   - **Cut:** a block of fields referred to only as "27 through 30" in the working sheet, and the raw Google Place ID (called out as not independently useful).
   - **Keep, lower priority:** missing-website flag, missing-phone flag.
3. **Doctor.com** — a healthcare-vertical listings product — sells only as an **add-on nested inside Local SEO** (specifically under the "Listing Pro"/Listing Sync Pro add-on), not as its own top-level Marketplace listing. This is a real discoverability gap: "a lot of our partners actually have encountered that... it's something that's really hard to know unless you understand that it's an add-on to local SEO, because you'd just assume it's a standalone" product. Even Uberall's own partner manager (Brandon Kuse) initially forgot the exact navigation path and assumed it was standalone.
4. Doctor.com ships its own listings scan tool (referred to on the call as a "listing scan tool" / "physician scan tool"), functionally similar to a Snapshot Report but scoped to a medical practice: it takes first name, last name, practice name, specialty, email, office phone, role, and address, then returns a **"health grade"** analysis of that practice's listings. Vendasta (Brandon Kuse + Ashley Colson) is working with doctor.com to gray-label a version of this tool a partner can host on their own website as a dedicated instance ("their very own instance of the scan tool"), mirroring how a comparable vendor (local IQ) already gives its own sales team a private version of a similar tool to use as a sales aid.
5. Margin/positioning context for why doctor.com isn't actively pushed: its margin profile is worse than Vendasta's own "owner-operated" listings products, so it isn't marketed through community, email, or webinars today — it stays a passive, easy-to-miss marketplace listing unless a partner already serves healthcare/physician clients and specifically goes looking for it.
6. Uberall terminology clarified mid-call: the product is **"Local Pages"** (not "local wages" — a recurring transcription/hearing mishap corrected by Ashley Colson) — individual per-location landing pages Uberall generates, used as an SEO/citation surface for multi-location or franchise clients.
7. Local Pages hosting/deployment has two modes with different onboarding lift:
   - Hosted on the client's own website via an embedded script/slug — requires getting embed access or credentials from the client; described as the heavier lift, especially combined with any CRM integration and initial automation setup layered on top.
   - Hosted externally by Uberall, with the client's site simply linking out to it — simpler to stand up since no embed access is required.
8. Not-yet-live roadmap item (listings-adjacent, also touches Conversations AI): pairing Conversations AI (chat) with Local Pages so an individual location page can carry a chat widget. Still at the written-proposal stage at call time — Brandon was drafting a document on the underlying partner relationship ("Tiger/Pistol") that covers this. Proposed rollout if greenlit: start with a pilot of 3–5 client accounts (with explicit account-access permission granted) to map the real workflow before any broader go-to-market, since the heaviest expected lift is the CRM integration and initial automation setup — expected to get easier once that initial setup is done.
9. Vendor-relationship note surfaced but not resolved: Uberall pays Vendasta a flat rate for this integration, and Brandon (Uberall's channel contact) raised uncertainty about how much partner-success support/co-marketing Uberall gets relative to a competing listings-data provider (Yext) — flagged as a question for Desiree to raise internally, not something settled on this call.

Out of scope for this note (belongs to snapshot-executive-reports): the same call's discussion of exporting the full ~18,000-account BigQuery dataset, the SEO/organic-ranking data-sharing compliance blocker, and the reporting/data-warehouse mechanics generally.
