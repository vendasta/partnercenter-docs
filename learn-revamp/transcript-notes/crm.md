## Date unknown — Jehan Zouak (Vendasta) with certified partners Klint Rudolph, Jeff Pierce, Joshua Iverson, Brett Prieskorn, Patrick Howell, Simon Papadopoulos, Chris Geer (certified-partner roundtable)

**Source:** [CALL] Certified-partner peer roundtable; CRM-relevant segment covers lead-routing automation gaps and CRM task-management complaints (call also covered SEO and AI Workforce topics, filed elsewhere).

1. **Lead-routing automation for the certified-partner directory.** An automation named "Certified Vendasta partner → Contact us form intake" pushes leads from the certified-partner directory's contact form into the partner's own CRM/system. It is deliberately **not** configured to email-notify the partner — the intent was for each partner to build their own notification step on top of it. Gotcha: if a partner never built that notification, leads sit invisibly in their CRM until Vendasta staff happens to notice and emails them manually. One partner (Brett) discovered his automation trigger was simply switched off, meaning he'd received leads with zero awareness. Fix: verify the automation is toggled on for your account, and add your own notify step (email/Slack/task) rather than relying on Vendasta to flag inbound leads.
2. A partner asked whether leads that land in the CRM via that automation carry a **record source or record type field** so you can identify where they came from. The CS host did not know and said she'd have to confirm with the platform team — unresolved as of this call, worth re-checking before assuming record-source tagging exists on this particular automation.
3. **CRM task management is a recognized weak spot.** Multiple advanced partners independently said creating and managing tasks inside the Vendasta CRM "was not very good" and still isn't — one said they had to build several custom automations just to make task assignment/tracking function acceptably. Nickname coined on the call: task manager is "the red-headed stepchild" of the platform.
4. Because of that gap, several certified partners run their actual sales pipeline in **Pipedrive** instead of the native CRM specifically for its task management (auto-syncs rep tasks to Google Calendar). One partner described a ~3–4 month effort to build a custom API + automation integration so that every time his sales team ran a Snapshot report, it would automatically push data via API into Pipedrive to create/update an organization and deal record — avoiding manual re-entry. He confirmed it was very difficult to get working ("that took me three, four months ago... it was so hard").
5. Design principle repeated by the group for CRM/automation work generally: minimize manual human entry into the CRM — "you use automations and workflows... you don't want humans going in there. You want the stuff to just happen automatically and then tell the human when they have a thing to do."

## Date unknown — Alistair George (Vendasta sales engineer) with Brian Hardy (partner) and Umair Khan (Vendasta)

**Source:** [CALL] Technical build call for a Vibe-built restaurant app ("Square Peg Pizzeria") needing CRM/contact-tagging and automation wiring (call is mostly Vibe/Supabase-specific — out of scope; CRM/automation-building content only is filed here).

1. **Build-vs-integrate decision walked through live:** whether to keep contact data in a separate database (here, Supabase backing a custom Vibe app) vs. using Vendasta's native CRM as the single source of truth. Framing offered: running two databases (a "master" plus one or more "child" copies) raises a data-integrity question that has to be a deliberate choice — "what happens when they disagree, which one is correct."
2. **Bulk-importing existing contacts:** Contacts → **Import**, upload a CSV to load records that already exist in an external system into the CRM.
3. **Custom fields:** built under **Administration → CRM objects**, creatable on both Contacts and Companies (name, description, field type). Use the Companies object when the underlying data has an organizational structure (e.g., a catering client that is a business, not an individual) — Contacts alone can't model that relationship.
4. **Cross-account limitation confirmed:** an automation/workflow built inside one account cannot be copied directly into another account — it must be rebuilt from scratch there. The one workaround/exception is setting up an **account template at the partner-center level** for future reuse; one-off automations don't carry over account to account.
5. **Automation building blocks for a webhook-driven "new lead → tag → nurture" flow:** trigger = Webhook received (with defined payload fields, e.g., first name, last name, email, type) → **Find contact** by email → branch: if found, send to the existing contact and start a specified campaign; if not found, create a new contact directly from the payload fields (using the dynamic-content/field-mapping picker rather than manual entry).
6. Branching logic in Vendasta's automation builder is called an **"eval branch"** (a partner compared it directly to GoHighLevel's if/then columns) — supports multiple parallel branches off one trigger (e.g., branch by request type: catering / fundraiser / large reservation). Alistair's explicit recommendation: consolidate near-duplicate workflows into **one** automation with tag-based branches rather than maintaining several nearly identical workflows, since "having lots of different little workflows gets complicated pretty quickly."
7. **Date-anchored automations (e.g., "day after" or "30 days later" messages):** a webhook fires immediately, so you can't natively delay-trigger off it. Working pattern demonstrated: store the relevant date in a custom CRM date field (e.g., "catering request date"), build a **List** filtered on that field (e.g., "date is yesterday"), and trigger the follow-up campaign off contacts landing in that list. Use a **"goal"** condition (e.g., "changes to the contact record") to auto-remove a contact from the list/sequence once they've re-engaged (e.g., booked again) — functions as a re-entry reset.
8. **Live-reproduced bug/gotcha:** when mapping a webhook payload into a new-contact-creation step, the automation builder refused a dynamic-content/date-field reference for a custom date field and instead forced a hardcoded date value. Alistair confirmed this looked like a bug or product oversight and said he'd escalate to the product team — worth re-checking whether dynamic date-field mapping works before relying on it in a similar build.
9. Comparative note volunteered for partners migrating from other platforms: "CRM-wise, it's quite similar — if you know GoHighLevel (GHL), you'll feel right at home."

## Date unknown — Heather Gatlin, Maeghan, Brian Heppner (Jungle/"Happiness" call-center client) with Logan Ochitwa & Andrew Claus (Vendasta) — technical RFP deep-dive on CRM + Kixie + ServiceMinder integration

**Source:** [CALL] `ai-receptionist-call-timing-configuration.txt` — CRM/Kixie/ServiceMinder integration-architecture portion only (the AI response snooze/wake-up configuration content from this same call is filed in `ai-workforce.md`).

1. **Vendasta CRM as the system of record, Kixie as the outbound calling/SMS layer on top of it:** inbound calls, chats, and SMS all flow into Vendasta's **Conversations** panel and attach to the matching CRM contact record (matched by phone number or email; if no match exists, a new contact record is created automatically). Outbound dialing/texting can happen either from Vendasta's own Conversations panel directly, or via a one-click launch into the Kixie soft-phone/auto-dialer, with Kixie activity pushed back into the same Vendasta CRM contact record either way.
2. **Inbound email sync via Yesware:** Vendasta acquired Yesware, which is the mechanism behind inbound/outbound email syncing into the CRM — a client's support/reply-to email address is tied into the CRM so all inbound email threads automatically match to and populate the corresponding contact record (or create a new one if no match exists).
3. **Custom Fields vs. Custom Objects — a real architectural distinction, not just naming:** a **custom field** hangs off an existing record type (Contact or Company) as a single data point. A **custom object** is an entirely separate data type/table for when the underlying data doesn't roll up naturally to a contact (the rep's example: a vet clinic's "pet" isn't a contact — it needs its own object with its own custom fields like vaccination record). The client on this call needed as many as ~42 distinct disposition/outcome categories modeled, which could be built either as 42 custom objects or as fields/values on a single custom field — a real design decision with data-volume and reporting implications.
4. **Pipelines are effectively unlimited, and dispositions can span multiple pipelines:** e.g., disposition values 1-10 mapped to an "MQL" pipeline, 11-20 to an "SQL" pipeline, 21-30 to a "support" pipeline — each with its own custom fields. Automations can trigger either off a **lifecycle-stage/custom-field change** (e.g., a disposition value changing on the contact record) or off an **opportunity moving to a different pipeline stage** — both are valid trigger points, and the practical decision is which one requires fewer clicks for the agent doing the work day-to-day (Vendasta's explicit recommendation: keep the agent working from wherever they already are — usually the contact record — rather than forcing a page switch).
5. **Smart Lists as the conditional-routing mechanism:** a **Smart List** auto-populates based on defined conditions (e.g., "disposition = X" or "zip code in region Y") and functions as a live queue — once a contact meets a Smart List's criteria it's added automatically, and an automation can be built to trigger off "contact added to this Smart List" (e.g., auto-enroll in a specific call queue or SMS cadence, or hold for N hours before re-attempting contact within business hours for that record's timezone).
6. **Automation self-cancellation / conditional re-check pattern:** Vendasta's automation engine re-validates trigger conditions at each wait step rather than assuming they still hold — example given: a contact enters a 12-hour wait step while in "pipeline A, disposition 6"; if by the time the wait elapses the contact has since moved to disposition 7, the automation detects the mismatch and cancels that flow rather than acting on stale state.
7. **Two-way CRM-to-external-CRM sync pattern (Vendasta ↔ ServiceMinder):** built as a mapping exercise between Vendasta's custom fields/objects and the external system's schema, pushed via API or webhook off a triggering event (an agent logging a note, running an automation manually, or any other defined trigger). Vendasta's team explicitly frames the reconciliation approach as "source of truth plus diffing" — designate one system (here, ServiceMinder) as authoritative, then match/reconcile and flag discrepancies rather than blindly overwriting either side.
8. **Recommended build sequence for a complex multi-system integration, stated as a general best practice:** start with the platform's own core CRM/automation functionality working correctly and validated in isolation *before* wiring in the external-system sync — "if our platform doesn't do the piece you need, it's kind of moot whether we can push/pull data from [the external system] the way you need." Tackle the most complex disposition/workflow case first rather than the simplest, on the reasoning that solving the hardest case first de-risks all the simpler ones; a mock/sandbox instance with fake data is used to validate the full flow before going live.

## Date unknown — Kent Falk (Vendasta) with Tom Coleman, Alexander Retzlik, Myron Kindrachuk (European/Swedish reseller building an outbound AI sales stack)

6. **Sales-cycle and contract-structure benchmarks shared by Vendasta as reference points:** typical close cycle for a full-suite sale is **3–5 weeks (20–40 days)**; partners selling only listings+reputation as a foot-in-the-door close faster (~2 weeks) but then have to work harder to upsell later; once a rep's pipeline is built up, ~40–50 warm leads per rep becomes the steady-state target. ## Date unknown — Jehan Zouak (Vendasta, roundtable host) & Patrick Howell (Howell Studios), with Brett Prieskorn, Joshua Iverson, Scott Regan, Klint Rudolph, Simon Papadopoulos (certified-partner roundtable)

**Source:** [CALL] `certified-partner-roundtable-ai-lead-qualification-seo.txt` — CRM opportunities-pipeline mechanics and lead-dedup portion of this call (the AI lead-qualification/enrichment content from the same "Lead Suite" walkthrough is already filed in `ai-workforce.md`).

1. **Opportunities used as a custom, many-stage automation backbone, not a standard 4–5-stage sales pipeline:** Patrick's build advances a lead through a much longer chain of Opportunity stages than the CRM's typical setup (well beyond "prospect → proposal → won/lost") specifically because every stage transition is wired to an automation — moving a lead into a stage is what triggers the next email/SMS/AI-outreach step. Multiple roundtable partners (Joshua, Brett) pushed back live that this is an unusually "robust" way to use Opportunities and said they'd normally solve the same problem with a numeric lead-score field driving automation instead of many pipeline columns — Patrick's reasoning for his approach: it's "more about the stickiness" (getting the client's own team to actually live inside the CRM) and it's operationally simpler for him to build automations off discrete stage changes than off a single score threshold.
2. **Stalled-opportunity automation pattern:** if a lead sits too long in a given stage without responding (his example: stuck in "sample kit requested"), an AI step is triggered to personally follow up and keep nudging until it gets a yes or no — rather than letting the opportunity go stale.
3. **Lead deduplication is handled upstream, before data ever reaches the CRM:** because leads are pulled from ~80 different paid data sources and multiple advertising/tracking channels (organic search, paid search, Advertising Intelligence, visual-visitor tools) simultaneously, Patrick's own external servers rinse and cross-reference every incoming record before pushing it into the client's CRM via API — his claim is the CRM itself never sees true duplicates, though near-duplicates (e.g., "Rob" vs. "Robert" with a different phone/email) can still slip through as separate contacts. A roundtable partner (Brett) specifically pressed on whether the same person could get double-counted once from an ads-tracking source and again from the intent-data pipeline; Patrick's answer was that the original acquisition channel is preserved and consolidated, not duplicated, on his server side.
4. **Record-source field used deliberately as a client-facing narrative-control lever:** the CRM's record-source column is what determines the label a client sees on their home-screen lead count (e.g., "Intent leads by [agency name]") — Patrick reported this mattered enough to his retention that he re-labeled/consolidated a source that was showing "DV" internally, specifically because an unlabeled or badly-labeled record source reads as untrustworthy to the end client, even when the underlying lead volume is solid.
5. **API access to a specific client account, clarified for a partner who'd been told otherwise:** a roundtable partner (Scott) said he'd been told Vendasta's API could only connect at the Partner Center level, not into individual client Business App accounts. Patrick corrected this live — his system pushes data into specific client CRMs via API by supplying that account's **PID plus AG number**, confirming account-level API write access is possible once you have those two identifiers, contrary to what Scott had been told elsewhere.
