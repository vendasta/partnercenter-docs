## Date unknown — Hadley Schmidt (Vendasta/managed-services rep) with Jamie Morrison (partner) — automation-setup follow-up call, Social Marketing product tour

**Source:** [CALL] `automation-setup-followup-technical-escalation.txt` — full Social Marketing product walkthrough (the automation-escalation and CS-internal-routing content from this same call is filed in `builder.md`).

1. **Product surface, tour order:** Overview (connected accounts, e.g. Facebook/GBP; scheduled posts for the next 30 days; recent posts from the last 30 days with reach/comments/engagement) → Posts (drafted, scheduled, and published, filterable by date range and platform) → Calendar (month-ahead view of everything scheduled, across platforms) → Tools (saved templates for consistent posting style; a "clickable bio" link-in-bio feature) → Comments (engagement management across connected platforms, plus an RSS-feed-subscribe feature for repost-worthy inspirational content, similar in spirit to LinkedIn reposts) → Insights (link-click performance originating from social, and post reach/engagement over time, filterable per platform) → Settings (account connections, RSS feeds, AI settings).
2. **Post types supported:** long-form posts, short video, stories, and multi-post "campaigns" that schedule several posts at once; a blog-post creation option also exists in the tool but the rep noted she'd never actually seen a partner use it.
3. **AI caption/content generation, demoed live:** typing a rough prompt (e.g., "Father's Day deal for Father Bunny and Baby Bunny") generates a ready caption; a **280-character limit** applies and the tool visibly flags/truncates captions that run long. An "AI generate image" option exists alongside caption generation for the same post.
4. **Client-facing approval workflow:** posts scheduled by the partner or their team surface inside the client's own Business App view, letting the end client see exactly what's queued for which day/platform and approve it there — framed as a key trust/transparency feature for clients who want visibility without doing the posting work themselves.
5. **Fulfillment split, stated plainly by the rep:** some partners buy Vendasta's own managed-services team to run this (an in-house marketing team producing the content), but the rep's direct experience is that most partners end up doing Social Marketing themselves because the AI-assisted workflow makes self-service fast enough that managed services isn't necessary for most accounts.
6. **Product bundling context volunteered in the same call:** Reputation and Social Marketing are commonly bundled together; CRM AI is called out as a good additional sell for small/newer companies who need built-in sales coaching for inexperienced reps (message-tone scoring, pitch feedback).

## Date unknown — Joshua Iverson, Brett Prieskorn, Chris Geer, Simon Papadopoulos, Patrick Howell, Jehan Zouak (certified partners) — roundtable discussion on Social AI adoption and product-organization concerns

**Source:** [CALL] `certified-partner-roundtable-seo-ai-workforce-crm.txt` — Social AI adoption/maturity discussion portion only (the SEO/Local-SEO-Listings and AI-Workforce/CRM portions of this same call are filed in `local-seo-listings.md`, `ai-workforce.md`, and `crm.md`).

1. **Live debate over whether "managed social media" services are quietly being replaced by the Social AI agent:** Brett directly asked whether Vendasta's own managed-services team is now just running the same Social AI agent behind the scenes rather than doing bespoke human content work — raised as a transparency concern for partners who sell "managed services" to their own clients without knowing exactly what's automated versus human-produced. Jehan (Vendasta) confirmed via a colleague that Vendasta Services itself has been using Social AI and related AI employees "for the past few months," suggesting the shift is real but not yet clearly communicated to partners.
2. **Partner adoption is real but still described as immature/"dabbling," not a firm directive** for several partners on the call — one partner's own team lead was "starting to use it more" but without a clear mandate to fully switch over.
3. **Concrete unmet product need raised by Joshua:** he wants Social Posting to also send a very simple monthly email newsletter (to directly replace Constant Contact for a specific client) without requiring heavy CRM automation setup — explicitly so he can price it as a low-cost, simple add-on (hundreds/month, not thousands) rather than justifying a full CRM/automation sale. As of this call, he considered the Blog Writer/Social AI/Blogger combination not yet capable of that simplified workflow.
4. **Concrete positive example of Social/Blog AI reinforcing subscription value, from Chris Geer:** a two-location childcare-center client emailed her own SEO keyword suggestions pulled from ChatGPT; rather than manually incorporating them, Chris showed the client her own dedicated, named/branded **AI Search employee** already built into her Business App and reminded her it exists specifically to do this kind of work — reinforcing to the client that tools she's already paying for cover what she was about to seek elsewhere, and opening an upsell conversation for additional blog/SEO work.
5. **Product-organization complaint raised by Brett** (relevant to information architecture, not just Social specifically): he argued the Social agent and Blogger agent shouldn't live inside the general "AI Workforce" dashboard at all — they should live inside the Social app itself (and, by the same logic, the SEO agent should live inside the SEO Pro app), with AI Workforce visibility as an optional admin toggle rather than the primary home for these product-specific agents.
