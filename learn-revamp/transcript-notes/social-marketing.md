## Date unknown — Patty H (Vendasta) with a partner's social media manager — Meta Business Suite "full access" gotcha blocking a Facebook page merge
**Source:** [CALL] `facebook-meta-business-suite-merge-troubleshooting.txt` — troubleshooting call for merging an old and new Facebook business page for the same client.

1. **Root cause of a stuck page merge:** merging two Facebook Business Pages requires the person doing the merge to have **"Full Access" specifically inside Meta Business Suite** for the older page's business portfolio — not just regular Facebook admin/page access, which the staff member already had. Facebook's merge option won't populate for someone without that specific access level, even if they can otherwise manage the page normally.
2. **Diagnosis method:** confirmed by checking the People/Partners section of the old page's Meta Business Suite — it showed the staff member with only "partial access," while others in that business portfolio had full access, explaining why the merge tool never appeared for her.
3. **Two possible fixes identified:** either (a) get granted full access within that older Meta Business Suite specifically, or (b) have whoever currently owns the old page's business portfolio **remove the page from their business portfolio entirely**, which reverts it to regular Facebook-only access and resolves the access mismatch that way.
4. **Confirmed independently by Facebook support:** support could see the staff member had admin access to the page itself, but confirmed the old page's business portfolio was owned by a different entity — matching the root cause identified above.
5. **Separate Instagram-connection gotcha caught on the same call:** an error reading *"Instagram messages are unavailable in your inbox until you confirm that people who manage your Facebook page can also manage your Instagram messages"* — even after the required confirmation step had already been completed — sometimes requires clicking the same confirmation link a second time before it actually takes effect.
6. **Reporting tip:** Meta Business Suite's **Insights** tab (roughly the sixth item down the left sidebar) gives a consolidated view of reach/engagement/interactions across the connected page — called out as more useful for a big-picture read than checking individual post metrics one at a time.

## Date unknown — Stephen Zerbs, Malu Sanchez (Vendasta/MatchCraft channel team) with Jordan H (Vendasta) — Facebook Page ownership-discovery workflow, taught for a reseller's Facebook Page Claiming fulfillment process
**Source:** [CALL] `facebook-page-access-workflow-training-spectrum-partner.txt` — an internal training call teaching a channel-partner team (fulfilling for reseller "Spectrum") how Facebook Page access requests actually resolve, ahead of scaling a Facebook Page Claiming product. (The MatchCraft-specific fulfillment-SKU mechanics from this same call are filed in `advertising.md`.)

1. **The core mechanic, demonstrated live:** after sending a page-access request from the platform, the requesting side sees one of two labels next to "owned by" — **"individual people"** (never names who) or **"[Business Manager Name]"** — and that label determines which of two completely different acceptance flows the end client has to follow.
2. **If owned by a Business Manager:** the client logs into **business.facebook.com**, and the Business Manager name shown at the top of their screen must match the name shown in the "owned by" label — if it doesn't match, there's no point continuing, since the request will never appear there. If it matches, the path is Settings → **Requests**, where the incoming access request appears for the client to approve/decline with a description of exactly what access level is being requested.
3. **If owned by "individual people":** the client instead has to log into their **personal Facebook**, switch into the correct Page (if they manage multiple), then go to Settings → Page Setup → Page access → **"Partners with access"**, where the same kind of request/approve prompt appears.
4. **Real ownership-ambiguity gotcha:** Facebook shows a list of people with admin/"Facebook access" to a page but never indicates which one is the true **owner** — sometimes only the actual creator/owner sees an incoming access request even though others also have admin access; other times, once the original owner has left the organization, all remaining admins start seeing the request. There is no direct way to determine ownership from within the tool itself.
5. **Real-world failure mode:** if the true owner has since left the company on bad terms and can't be reached, and no other admin sees the access request, the only remaining path is a formal **Facebook ownership-dispute process** — a slow, no-clean-workaround scenario worth setting client expectations around up front.
6. **Access-level distinction worth knowing:** people with only **"Task access"** (a lower tier than full Facebook/admin access) can never approve an access request regardless of what task-level permissions they hold — no point escalating to them.
7. **Practical recommendation that came out of the training:** rather than have every partner or reseller support rep explain this two-branch flow live to confused clients, record and share just this walkthrough segment as a standalone reference video, since the mechanics themselves don't change per client.

## Date unknown — Terri H (Vendasta/Broadly) with Gil Calkins and Julie (client) — creating brand-new X/Twitter and Instagram accounts from scratch during onboarding
**Source:** [CALL] `social-media-onboarding-x-instagram-clean-vent-dryer-gil-calkins.txt` — a social-onboarding call where the client had no prior X or Instagram presence at all.

1. **New-account creation, start to finish:** using a Gmail address the client had already set up specifically for social management, the rep created both a new X account and a new Instagram account live on the call, verifying via one-time email codes and X's required **date-of-birth field** (used by the platform to confirm the account holder is 18+).
2. **Handle/name-length constraint:** X rejected the full official business name for the handle/display name (too many characters, and adding a descriptive suffix like "exhaust specialist" pushed it over) — resolved by using a shortened version of the business name only.
3. **Category-selection constraint:** X's fixed category list didn't include the client's specific niche (dryer-vent cleaning); the rep had to pick the closest available generic bucket ("Home and Garden"/"Professional Services") since custom categories aren't allowed — the client separately noted this same category gap exists on other directories too (a competitor listing platform had to create a whole new category for this niche when he first started the business years ago).
4. **Bio-writing approach:** wrote a keyword-rich business description within X's character limit (160 characters), deliberately working in service type + years in business + service area, then live-negotiated specific wording with the client (e.g., correcting an inaccurate founding-year claim, softening a "family owned" framing the client wasn't sure he wanted to emphasize, broadening the stated service area from one city to "Greater [Region]").
5. **Password-sharing practice:** the rep set (and read out, then emailed) a single shared password across both new platforms specifically to keep it simple for the client to also log in, respond to comments/DMs directly, and monitor the accounts themselves — deliberately made "stronger" than the client's first suggestion once the client pointed out his own first guess was too guessable.
6. **Cross-platform content tailoring set as an expectation up front:** the same underlying post will look different per platform — Facebook gets more detail and a website link, X is capped to roughly two lines with no link — explained proactively so the client isn't confused later about why the same campaign looks different across platforms.
7. **Instagram-Facebook connection gotcha, unresolved at end of call:** connecting the newly-created Instagram account to the client's existing Facebook Page repeatedly failed at the final "confirm connection" step with a generic *"your request couldn't be processed"* error, despite following the standard Meta Business Suite Settings → Page Setup → Link Accounts → Instagram Connect flow correctly multiple times — logged as something to check again the next day rather than resolved live.
8. **Facebook admin access already in place:** the client had made "Broadly" (the reseller) an administrator on the existing Facebook page long before this call, which meant Facebook itself connected cleanly even while the Instagram link kept failing — a useful signal that a stuck cross-platform Instagram connection isn't necessarily an access-permission problem.

## Date unknown — Sayda Momtaha H, Mazharul M (Vendasta) with Ryan (Cerberus Digital, partner) — content-calendar draft-vs-scheduled workflow fix and account-notes practice
**Source:** [CALL] `partner-checkin-communication-workflow-cerberus-digital.txt` — a recurring partner check-in addressing a specific broken content-approval workflow and a missed SLA commitment.

1. **Lead-time principle, stated memorably by the partner:** content/marketing needs planning lead time the same way a seasonal campaign does — "if I came to you in November wanting a Black Friday campaign, you'd say I was two months late" — applied here to argue that a monthly content calendar or landing page realistically needs **10 days to two weeks** of runway through partner review → client review → feedback before it needs to go live.
2. **Concrete broken workflow, diagnosed and fixed live:** the fulfillment team had been publishing social posts directly to **"Scheduled"** status rather than leaving them in **"Draft,"** so when the partner clicked into the calendar link he landed on an empty drafts view with no way to mark anything "approved" or "needs edits" — his only option was editing the post in place, which defeated the point of paying for content creation ("well, I can create them there too"). **Fix agreed:** going forward, all calendars for this partner are delivered in Draft status specifically so there's a real review step; a self-service toggle back to auto-Scheduled doesn't exist, but the partner can request it once he's ready to fully hand off review (a transition path he specifically wants for weaning his own clients off needing to approve things too).
3. **Root cause of an earlier missed date-commitment:** the specific SLA date promised to this partner (calendars delivered by the 15th of the prior month) had only ever lived in one staff member's memory/inbox, not in a persistent, shared record — when that staff member's coverage changed, the commitment didn't carry over. **Fix:** any partner-specific commitment now gets logged in the account's **Project/Account Notes** specifically so it survives staff turnover rather than depending on one person remembering it.
4. **Recurring phone-routing frustration (same theme as this partner's other calls in this batch):** described getting stuck in an AI-receptionist "chases its tail" loop trying to reach a live human by phone, even after the AI's response latency had reportedly already been adjusted specifically to give staff more time to intercept calls — still insufficient in practice; the workaround was manually calling support and asking for a callback from a specific named staff member.
5. **Strategic signal worth tracking, volunteered by the partner:** described steadily moving accounts off Vendasta-managed social/blog services toward in-house or AI/Vibe-assisted alternatives (down from roughly 10-15 accounts to just two blogs and two socials remaining at the time of this call), citing peer-agency adoption of AI-built landing pages (in Divi 5) as a competitive pressure he's watching closely — framed as an open strategic question about whether Vendasta's own content/website tooling is keeping pace, not a definitive complaint. Account manager's response: reaffirmed that even a small remaining footprint gets full-quality attention, not deprioritized because volume dropped.
