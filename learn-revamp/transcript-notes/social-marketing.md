## Date unknown — Patty H (Vendasta) with a partner's social media manager — Meta Business Suite "full access" gotcha blocking a Facebook page merge
**Source:** [CALL] `facebook-meta-business-suite-merge-troubleshooting.txt` — troubleshooting call for merging an old and new Facebook business page for the same client.

1. **Root cause of a stuck page merge:** merging two Facebook Business Pages requires the person doing the merge to have **"Full Access" specifically inside Meta Business Suite** for the older page's business portfolio — not just regular Facebook admin/page access, which the staff member already had. Facebook's merge option won't populate for someone without that specific access level, even if they can otherwise manage the page normally.
2. **Diagnosis method:** confirmed by checking the People/Partners section of the old page's Meta Business Suite — it showed the staff member with only "partial access," while others in that business portfolio had full access, explaining why the merge tool never appeared for her.
3. **Two possible fixes identified:** either (a) get granted full access within that older Meta Business Suite specifically, or (b) have whoever currently owns the old page's business portfolio **remove the page from their business portfolio entirely**, which reverts it to regular Facebook-only access and resolves the access mismatch that way.
4. **Confirmed independently by Facebook support:** support could see the staff member had admin access to the page itself, but confirmed the old page's business portfolio was owned by a different entity — matching the root cause identified above.
5. **Separate Instagram-connection gotcha caught on the same call:** an error reading *"Instagram messages are unavailable in your inbox until you confirm that people who manage your Facebook page can also manage your Instagram messages"* — even after the required confirmation step had already been completed — sometimes requires clicking the same confirmation link a second time before it actually takes effect.
6. **Reporting tip:** Meta Business Suite's **Insights** tab (roughly the sixth item down the left sidebar) gives a consolidated view of reach/engagement/interactions across the connected page — called out as more useful for a big-picture read than checking individual post metrics one at a time.

## Date unknown — Stephen Zerbs, Malu Sanchez (Vendasta/MatchCraft channel team) with Jordan H (Vendasta) — Facebook Page ownership-discovery workflow, taught for a reseller's Facebook Page Claiming fulfillment process
**Source:** [CALL] `facebook-page-access-workflow-training-spectrum-partner.txt` — an internal training call teaching a channel-partner team (fulfilling for reseller "Spectrum") how Facebook Page access requests actually resolve, ahead of scaling a Facebook Page Claiming product. (The MatchCraft-specific fulfillment-SKU mechanics from this same call are filed in `advertising.md`.)

1. **The core mechanic, demonstrated live:** after sending a page-access request from the platform, the requesting side sees one of two labels next to "owned by" — **"individual people"** (never names who) or **"[Business Manager Name]"** — and that label determines which of two completely different acceptance flows the end client has to follow.
2. **If owned by a Business Manager:** the client logs into **business.facebook.com**, and the Business Manager name shown at the top of their screen must match the name shown in the "owned by" label — if it doesn't match, there's no point continuing, since the request will never appear there. If it matches, the path is Settings → **Requests**, where the incoming access request appears for the client to approve/decline with a description of exactly what access level is being requested.
3. **If owned by "individual people":** the client instead has to log into their **personal Facebook**, switch into the correct Page (if they manage multiple), then go to Settings → Page Setup → Page access → **"Partners with access"**, where the same kind of request/approve prompt appears.
4. **Real ownership-ambiguity gotcha:** Facebook shows a list of people with admin/"Facebook access" to a page but never indicates which one is the true **owner** — sometimes only the actual creator/owner sees an incoming access request even though others also have admin access; other times, once the original owner has left the organization, all remaining admins start seeing the request. There is no direct way to determine ownership from within the tool itself.
5. **Real-world failure mode:** if the true owner has since left the company on bad terms and can't be reached, and no other admin sees the access request, the only remaining path is a formal **Facebook ownership-dispute process** — a slow no-clean-workaround scenario worth setting client expectations around up front.
6. **Access-level distinction worth knowing:** people with only **"Task access"** (a lower tier than full Facebook/admin access) can never approve an access request regardless of what task-level permissions they hold — no point escalating to them.
7. **Practical recommendation that came out of the training:** rather than have every partner or reseller support rep explain this two-branch flow live to confused clients, record and share just this walkthrough segment as a standalone reference video, since the mechanics themselves don't change per client.
