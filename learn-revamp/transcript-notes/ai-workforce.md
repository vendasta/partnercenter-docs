## Date unknown — Brett Prieskorn (partner, Black Feather Digital) & Arsh Sandhu (Vendasta CS rep)

**Source:** [CALL] A2P compliance re-review and a chatbot legal-name bug, on a routine partner CS check-in (call also touched Vibe subdomains — filed separately)

Mixed CS check-in call; most of the substance is A2P/website compliance and one specific AI-chatbot bug.

1. **Partner Center AI agents cannot be attached to an email inbox — only Business App agents can.** The partner wanted an AI agent to sit on a shared support email address (triage incoming client emails, auto-respond, route to the right person). CS confirmed: in Partner Center, only the built-in chat receptionist can be connected to an email address; a custom AI agent/assistant built in Partner Center has no native email binding. That capability exists only in Business App. Getting email-triage behavior in Partner Center would require a manual Automation with trigger conditions on incoming email content, routed to a human/agent — not a native AI-agent-to-inbox connection.
2. **Practical workaround the partner landed on:** because Partner Center agents can't take email, split AI employees by surface — keep Partner Center AI agents as customer-support-facing (handling internal/client support queries) and use Business App AI agents as sales/new-prospect-facing, trained differently for each audience. CS's advice: don't over-complicate this split further than necessary.
3. **"One platform" migration risk for AI agents:** partner asked whether AI employees built today in Partner Center would merge or carry over once Vendasta unifies onto a single platform (internally discussed for ~3 years, no committed ETA — CS estimated "a year or two" at best). Data transfer is planned, but there's no confirmed answer on whether an AI agent's built-up configuration/training would merge with a separately-built Business App agent or need to be rebuilt as two agents. Practical implication flagged by the partner: building both now means expecting to maintain two separate chat receptionists rather than one, at least until (and possibly after) any platform consolidation.
4. **A2P resubmission came back "partially compliant"** — Vendasta's internal compliance report (run by a specific team member, described as more detailed than the standard report) flagged the SMS opt-in consent checkbox as missing specifically on a **Form**, not on a static page. A Vendasta-branded form embed normally carries the "I give consent" checkbox automatically; a custom-built form on the site did not inherit it and needed the checkbox added manually.
5. **Standalone SMS terms page is required even if SMS terms are already mentioned inside the general privacy policy** — mentioning SMS/texting terms only within the privacy policy is not sufficient for full compliance; a separate, standalone SMS terms/consent page is what the review checks for.
6. **Website support scope:** Vendasta web support can make the compliance fixes on a client site if the site is already in Website Pro (WordPress hosting). Importing an external, non-Vendasta-hosted WordPress site into Website Pro costs a flat **$200 per site** — that fee is separate from ongoing web support/maintenance.
7. **Chatbot gotcha — AI surfaces the legal DBA name from A2P registration instead of the branded business name, specifically in the SMS opt-in confirmation line.** A web chat widget for a business branded "Patio Shade" (awnings company) kept asking "Do you agree to receive text messages from Double JP Investments?" — the legal entity name from A2P registration, not the DBA. This happened despite: (a) an explicit "business name conventions" document in the AI's knowledge base instructing it to never use the legal name and always use the DBA, and (b) the same instruction added directly into the AI's role/prompt. A full prompt-content scan confirmed the legal-name string does not appear anywhere in the AI's prompt or knowledge files, so the leak isn't coming from AI configuration.
8. **Troubleshooting attempted and all failed:** disabling then re-enabling the SMS/text capability on the agent; deactivating and reactivating the WordPress chat-widget plugin; testing in an incognito browser session. None of these fixed the behavior — the legal name kept appearing in the opt-in line every time.
9. **Working theory / escalation:** suspected an undocumented backend link between the client's A2P registration record (which stores the legal name for Twilio purposes) and the Inbox Pro web chat widget's opt-in confirmation text, which appears to pull from the registration record rather than the AI's business-name knowledge for that one specific line. Escalated to Vendasta's internal "AI optimization team" to fix directly, since it isn't something the partner can override from the AI's own configuration.
10. **Resubmission mechanics reconfirmed:** after making the fixes, the partner has to wait for A2P to reject or approve the resubmission again before knowing if it worked — consistent with the general A2P pattern (each resubmission is a real wait, not an instant recheck).

## Date unknown — Kevin Hinahon (Vendasta account manager), Taylor & Mallory Peek (multi-brand franchise marketing partner)

**Source:** [CALL] Chatbot rollout and dashboard migration planning across six-plus franchise brands (Elysium legacy dashboard to Vendasta dashboard)

Account-management call about migrating and launching AI chat receptionists across a multi-brand franchise portfolio (brands mentioned: Dramatic, Monster Tree, Duty Calls, Mr. Sparky, Mosquito Squad, Benjamin Franklin, ASP, Home Watch, TCA, Screen Mobile, Lawn Squad, Wolfies, Junk Loggers).

Goal: move every brand's chatbot into the single Vendasta dashboard so the team stops needing two separate bookmarked logins.
2. **Conversation history does not migrate between dashboards.** Vendasta could not confirm a way to carry chatbot conversation history over when moving a bot from Elysium to the Vendasta dashboard — legal/compliance risk if history is needed later. Workaround: **export the conversation log from Elysium before or independent of the cutover.** The export format is a raw text dump per message (not "pretty" or human-formatted) — each row is one message with a message ID and a body field that, on inspection, turned out to already be an AI-generated summary of the exchange rather than the verbatim back-and-forth in at least one sampled case. The partner considered this acceptable for their legal/audit purposes as long as it's retrievable.
3. **The Elysium account itself is not being deleted** — it was moved to a free tier, and conversations continue to live there and remain accessible indefinitely unless Vendasta gives advance notice before any future deletion/data removal, at which point the partner would export first.
4. **Rollout sequencing / cohorting:** rather than launching all new brand chatbots at once, the partner batched them — first wave (Home Watch, TCA, Screen Mobile) targeted for the 24th, second wave (Lawn Squad, Wolfies, Junk Loggers) for the 31st, and a previously-disabled brand (ASP) re-enabled sometime in between with an updated FDD. Account manager called this batching "more manageable" as a rollout pattern.
5. **Geographic targeting configuration:** all new chatbots keep the existing convention of targeting by **state**, not zip code — the bot says something like "there's availability in this state; for information on your specific market, connect with our sales team." One brand (Monster Tree) already uses **zip-code-level** targeting instead and the partner may test moving one or two more brands from state- to zip-level targeting after the initial batch launches, but wants everything live on state-level targeting first.
6. **CRM integration via Zapier/webhook, per brand:** lead data flows from the chatbot into HubSpot through a Zapier zap using a **catch-hook function**; onboarding a new brand into the pipeline is "swap out the brand" in the existing zap — the brand name is what changes (labeled internally as "FD Source"), the rest of the structure/process stays identical across brands. Testing recommendation: run a couple of test submissions per new brand after setup to confirm data populates and maps correctly in HubSpot before going live.
7. **Access provisioning gotcha:** adding new brands to the dashboard access list requires someone to manually add each new site under a WordPress-user role so a specific person (in this case the account manager) can install/manage the chat widget on that brand's site — for the six new brands being launched, this had not yet been done and needed to happen before deployment.
8. **Pricing/billing mechanics for a multi-brand rollout:** the confirmed rate quoted was **$35/month per brand**, plus a separate one-time onboarding/launch fee per brand. Invoicing: brands can be consolidated into **one monthly invoice as long as it's broken out by line item per brand**; net-45 payment terms; ACH direct-payment as the collection method.
9. **Adoption proof point shared by the partner:** across the live brands so far, one closed deal has come directly from a chatbot lead, and additional paid-ad traffic landing on brand sites is engaging with the chatbot — enough that the partner's sales team is asking to accelerate the rollout to remaining brands ("when is it my turn?").

## Date unknown — Braedon Gaudet (Vendasta AI specialist) with Brooks McClary & Jeff Leach (BNI franchise client)

**Source:** [CALL] AI voice receptionist live demo plus a scoping discussion on when to use voice vs. SMS/email for outreach and re-engagement automation

Discovery/demo call ahead of a technical scoping meeting; call happened close to the Canada Day/July 4 holiday window based on scheduling references.

1. **Live voice demo walkthrough:** Vendasta called a live AI voice receptionist number for a fake test account (a "rabbit grooming" business) so the client could hear it cold. The AI answered generically, asked what it could help with, described services (nail trims, ear cleaning, coat brushing, boarding) and grooming supply retail, quoted a real price ("basic grooming including coat care starts around $40"), and moved into booking by asking for first and last name plus email "all at once" when the caller confirmed interest.
2. **Latency is the single biggest thing that needs tuning on a new voice AI Employee.** The demo call was noticeably slower to respond in the first exchanges (estimated 5-6 seconds) and got faster as the call progressed. The response/"thinking" time is directly adjustable in configuration — you can limit how much reasoning time the AI is given before it must respond, trading a bit of accuracy for lower perceived lag. By contrast, the **knowledge base** (what the AI knows about the business) is described as strong out of the box since it's learned directly from the account/business info — voice call-flow tuning is where the real setup effort goes, not knowledge quality.
3. **Voice selection:** Vendasta's voice AI partners with **ElevenLabs** for text-to-speech voices — you can browse and select different preset voices (male/American, male/Canadian-leaning accents were shown) directly in the AI Employee configuration. Custom celebrity-soundalike voices (client jokingly asked for a "Sam Elliott" or "Morgan Freeman" style voice) are not available out of the box.
4. **Call-capture behavior:** if a caller doesn't complete the booking flow (e.g., hangs up before giving name/number), the conversation still logs under Conversations as a phone call record, and the caller may receive a follow-up text prompting for their info if it wasn't captured live on the call.
5. **Outbound AI voice calling exists but is in development** and was explicitly flagged as not yet something to lean on for outreach: "if I got a call from AI, I'm not responding — I'm hanging up. I can tell right away." The clear guidance from the AI specialist: don't use AI voice for outreach/cold engagement; voice is much stronger for **inbound service and triage** ("like a call-tree — press one for this, press two for that — AI is way better at that").
6. **Channel-selection framework given on the call (voice vs. SMS vs. email):**
   - Use **text/SMS for initial outreach and re-engagement** — people don't answer unknown calls and often don't read email, but they will at least glance at a text. Cited stat: SMS review requests see roughly an **80% bump in response rate over email**.
   - Use **voice as an AI receptionist for inbound service** — described as "a hundred times better than no receptionist," handling call routing/transfers (e.g., transferring to a specific person by name), lead capture, and guiding callers who don't know exactly who to ask for.
   - A workable blended pattern: start outreach via text, and have the AI's text message explicitly offer "if you'd prefer to talk, call this number" — letting the recipient self-select into a live AI voice conversation rather than forcing voice first.
   - AI voice can also be positioned as an information/routing agent for inbound calls once a relationship exists (e.g., a caller who already attended an event can ask about related opportunities and get transferred to the right specific contact, if transfer capabilities/rules are configured for that).
7. **Capability building block referenced:** transfer-to-a-person capability can guide a caller directly to a named individual instead of a generic phone menu, once that routing is configured on the AI Employee.

## Date unknown — Alistair George (Vendasta engineering) & Mario Cyril (Vendasta CSM), internal working session

**Source:** [CALL] Internal deep-dive on "Vector," Vendasta's internal AI employee used for CS/partner escalation routing, plus a company-wide push for AI Employee reporting

This is an internal engineering/CS working session (not a partner call), but it directly covers AI Employee architecture, capability/tool design, escalation logic, and reporting expectations that generalize to how any AI Employee is built and debugged — in scope per the task list.

1. **Architecture confirmed: "the only type of AI employee that there is, is Conversations" now** — all AI employees (including "Vector," the internal assistant used in a Google Chat escalation channel) are built on the assistants + Conversations framework; older, separate AI-employee mechanisms no longer exist.
2. **Custom capability + custom tool pattern, concretely walked through:** a capability called "VMF rep lookup" has a prompt telling the AI what it's for, plus an attached **tool that calls a Google Cloud Function** (built and maintained by one engineer, outside any internal low-code tooling — "I went rogue, I didn't use [internal Vibe/automation tooling], I just did it the way I knew how"). The tool passes a "chat user info" field (essentially just user ID, name, and email — described as "super, super thin" context) to the Cloud Function, which then does the lookups: user ID to account/pitch, pitch to CRM company, CRM company to assigned salesperson, salesperson to their booking-link field (buried in a partner's own back-end profile settings, in a spot most partners never find on their own).
3. **Escalation tool design metaphor (useful for teaching capability design generally):** the "Escalate" tool was deliberately built to require minimal reasoning from the AI — described as "a big red button": when the AI decides a conversation needs human help, it just presses the button and the Cloud Function handles all the downstream logic (finding the right person, posting to the right Google Chat channel). The AI doesn't need complex branching logic; keep the decision the AI makes simple, and offload the resolution logic to the tool/backend.
4. **Known bugs/gotchas surfaced in this review, useful as general AI Employee troubleshooting lessons:**
   - Escalations were still routing to a departed employee whose name remained hardcoded/stale in CRM records — a reminder that lookup tools are only as good as the underlying CRM data hygiene.
   - A "leadership group is not assigned" / "default routes unconfigured" error appeared when the user-ID lookup failed to match — there was no graceful fallback (e.g., surfacing a support email to the partner); it just posted an "nobody found" message to the internal escalation channel. Flagged as needing a proper fallback path.
   - The prompt itself had accumulated redundant, hardcoded reference data (a manually maintained list of account managers'/CSMs' names) that duplicated what the lookup tool already fetches dynamically — a sign of prompt bloat/drift after multiple rounds of ad hoc edits by different people, and a concrete argument for periodically auditing and trimming an AI Employee's prompt rather than only ever adding to it.
   - **LLMs cannot literally hold a timer** — the prompt contained an instruction like "wait 8 minutes, and if the partner hasn't responded, close the ticket." The engineer's clarification: an LLM does get a timestamp of when a message was sent, but it has no way to natively "wait" a fixed duration and then act — trying to make a language model behave like a deterministic timer/cron job is a design mistake, not a prompting problem to solve with better wording.
5. **A hidden internal testing/mock environment exists** for validating changes to an AI Employee before they go live: a "platform channel" wired into Business App lets someone message the AI Employee directly in a test/mock context with the same tools active, so prompt or capability edits can be validated without touching the real, partner-facing chat surface. This was built as an intentional R&D feature (distinct from a similar-looking one-off integration built elsewhere just as a favor for a single internal stakeholder) and requires an engineering ticket to extend or port elsewhere, since it needs backend work to give an AI Employee that same level of conversational context.
6. **Extending a Cloud-Function-backed tool to write, not just read:** the same tool architecture used for read-only lookups (find the rep, find the booking link) could be extended to write actions — e.g., logging a CRM note on a company record, or triggering a downstream automation that creates a support ticket automatically from an AI-detected escalation, rather than only pinging a Google Chat channel for a human to act on manually. Flagged as a near-term roadmap idea, not yet built.
7. **Company-wide reporting/analytics mandate on AI Employees:** leadership (with a "big push" cited around Cal Cooper's role) wants **reporting built for every AI Employee**, not just one internal tool — the expectation is that whatever reporting gets built for the internal "Vector" assistant should generalize as a pattern other AI Employees can reuse, since architecturally they all now run on the same Conversations-based assistant framework.

## Date unknown — Jeff Leach, Braedon Gaudet, Logan Ochitwa (Vendasta) & Edith Wiseman, Ambika Oberoi (FranData)

**Source:** [CALL] Scoping call for a custom AI Employee to validate franchise-owner phone numbers via outbound AI voice/SMS/email, with Kixie as the telephony partner

Discovery/scoping call for a bespoke outbound-validation AI Employee, not a standard AI receptionist — useful for understanding how Vendasta scopes a custom AI SDR build and the guardrails around outbound AI voice.

1. **"AI SDR" (AI Sales Development Rep) is Vendasta's existing outbound-calling AI product, in beta at the time of this call.** It can run a full conversation trying to book a meeting, or be stripped down to a much simpler yes/no data-confirmation call ("we're just validating that this is your correct information — yes or no") — the reduced use case here didn't need the AI to book anything, just confirm or correct a data point and end the call.
2. **Proposed use case:** validate a database of franchise-owner phone numbers (whether the number still works and whether it's a cell, home, or office line) across a target list that could range from ~25,000 (highest-value/full-contact-info owners, the recommended starting subset) up to 200,000 total contacts. The trigger for wanting this: customer demand has shifted hard toward phone-number accuracy over the last few years ("before, people didn't care about phones, all they wanted was email — now people are like, I don't care about email, all I want is a phone number").
3. **Proposed multi-channel outreach pattern:** AI places the outbound call first; if it reaches a voicemail, capture the voicemail greeting text/audio and check whether it names the target person (an identity-validation signal); if the call isn't answered or goes to a screening service, automatically follow up with an SMS in a human-sounding voice ("Hi, it's [name] from [org], just confirming this is still the right number for [target]. Reply yes/no.").
4. **Confidence-score model proposed instead of a binary right/wrong flag:** a match in the existing database starts a low confidence score; a successfully connected call increases it; a voicemail greeting that names the correct person increases it further; a live pickup with the person confirming their own identity brings it to 100%. This avoids treating data validation as strictly pass/fail when a lot of real-world signals are partial.
5. **Post-call AI conversation analysis is an existing capability**: the AI can be taught to read the transcript of a call afterward and extract structured outcomes (did the person say yes, did they say no, did the call actually connect, did they react negatively) rather than requiring a rigid press-1/press-2 menu structure — described as already available ("we have a component on our end where we can do post-call AI conversation analysis").
6. **"Prospector" is Vendasta's internal contact-enrichment tool** (from the Yesware acquisition, a Google Workspace/Outlook extension for email campaign tracking): a ~200-million-record database searchable by name, phone, email, company, or business category, returning name/title/company/location/email/phone/LinkedIn — proposed here as an extra cross-reference data point to help validate or find contact info, not as a standalone source of truth (a live demo in the call showed real mismatches — wrong company matched by name — underscoring it needs human/AI judgment layered on top, not blind trust).
7. **Data-privacy/AI-model assurances given to the (understandably data-protective) prospect:** Vendasta holds enterprise licenses with all major frontier model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini), meaning customer data does not flow back to those vendors for training, logging, or storage; partner data is siloed into separate partner instances; cited proof point: Vendasta's largest partner (described as one of the largest cybersecurity companies in the world) put them through a full security/compliance audit before signing, and passed.
8. **Regulatory nuance on outbound AI calling flagged as a real, current gap:** Canada currently has fewer consumer-protection restrictions on outbound automated calling than the US, a reversal of the historical pattern — the Vendasta rep noted he personally screens robocalls in Canada by muting and waiting for a live human voice before engaging, since fully automated dialers wait for the callee to speak first.
9. **Messaging gotcha:** opening an AI validation call with a blunt identity question ("Hi, is this [full name]?") tends to trigger reflexive suspicion/pushback ("who wants to know?") rather than a clean yes/no — leading with a stated reason first ("I'm calling to do a quick data-validation survey — do I have the right number for [name]?") gets a much more usable response.
10. **Commercial structure for a custom AI Employee engagement of this kind:** typically a monthly platform/subscription minimum plus usage-based pricing (per minute of talk time, per SMS sent, per email monitored/scraped), with per-unit pricing dropping sharply at volume; standard minimum term is one year, though Vendasta often proposes 3-year agreements with a 1-year opt-out so pricing is locked for three years without needing to re-run legal review annually. New custom builds like this typically start with a **30/60/90-day pilot phase** against a defined subset (e.g., 1,000 contacts) with explicit KPIs (e.g., a target percentage successfully validated across all channels) before the full contract and full data set are unlocked.
11. **Kixie is the dedicated telephony/phone-number partner, not Vendasta itself** — described as "a very deep and tight partner... it's what we use internally" — Vendasta builds and owns the AI/employee logic layer, Kixie supplies the outbound calling/number infrastructure, and a build like this would be scoped jointly with Kixie to confirm daily call-volume/number limits.

## Date unknown — Nathan Yaworski (Vendasta marketing services) & Zulfiquar N (Vendasta AI build team), with Bart Foreman & Phillip Brooks (partners, hair-salon vertical: Sola Salons and Salon Visage)

**Source:** [CALL] End-to-end AI voice receptionist build and A2P onboarding walkthrough for a multi-location, multi-brand hair salon suite-rental network

Extremely detailed build call — matches the target depth closely and is one of the richest sources for AI receptionist setup mechanics.

1. **Each brand/client's AI Employee gets its own dashboard and knowledge base inside its own Business App instance** — you do not build multiple client brands' AI employees inside one shared demo account; a demo account is only for showing capability, the real build happens in the client's own instance once turned on.
2. **Keep "knowledge" and "workflow" as two separate uploaded documents when handing off a build.** Knowledge = static facts (hours, pricing, locations, links, booking-system URLs). Workflow = the actual conversation logic/branching (what questions to ask, in what order, what to do with each answer). Mixing workflow into the knowledge doc doesn't work — workflow belongs in the AI's capabilities, not its knowledge base, and keeping them separate makes it much easier for a build team to plug knowledge straight in.
3. **Deliberate "keep it simple" design choice on booking:** rather than have the AI try to give subjective advice (e.g., what haircut style/length works for a caller), the AI's job is just to capture basic intent (what service, what location) and text back a link to the existing online booking system for the caller to complete themselves — avoids the AI making judgment calls it can't reliably make, especially for first-time callers with no history in the system.
4. **Two different workflows for two different business models on the same platform:**
   - **Suite-rental model ("Sola"):** caller states what they need (e.g., a kids' haircut); the AI looks up a database of local independent stylists offering that service and texts back a shortlist — deliberately capped at 5 results even when 12 qualify, to avoid an overwhelming "Dead Sea Scroll" of options.
   - **Staffed-salon model ("Salon Visage"):** ~80% of calls are simple booking/cancellation (handled via a booking link) and ~20% are miscellaneous (e.g., "running late" — AI asks how many minutes, notifies the front desk, sends a confirmation text). More complex service requests (color, chemical treatments, extensions) are explicitly routed to "please call in" rather than the automated booking flow, because the client's point-of-sale/booking system only supports simple-service self-booking online — this is a plain instruction rule ("if the caller mentions color, tell them they have to call and give them the number"), not a complex judgment call.
5. **Custom internal API + round-robin distribution, built specifically because no client API existed:** a custom API was built to return exactly 5 matching stylists per service/location request on a rotating basis (next caller gets the next 5 in sequence, cycling back to the start) so lead flow doesn't always funnel to the same 5 people — explicitly requested because manually rotating referrals "would defeat the whole purpose of helping the suite renders get new business."
6. **Staff-turnover gotcha, unresolved without a client-side API:** without a live connection to the client's own staffing records, if a stylist leaves, the AI has no way to know and could keep referring/texting a disconnected number. Resolution: without an API tie-in, roster updates must be done **manually** by the build team going forward — and this practical reality is exactly why a monthly managed service (someone gets paid to keep it current) was recommended over a pure one-time setup, for any client whose staffing changes often.
7. **Multi-location handling pattern:** one AI agent can serve several locations by structuring one knowledge "column" of info per location, with the very first thing the AI asks any caller being "what location are you looking for?" — so the right location's data gets used for the rest of the call. Location counts per phone number don't need to match cleanly (one owner in this account has two phone numbers, one routing to 6 locations, the other to 7) — "it never will be the same and we don't care" was the explicit design philosophy.
8. **Product-tier rule when one number serves multiple locations:** that scenario requires **Conversations AI Premium**, purchased once per distinct phone number (not per location).
9. **Deliberate privacy design choice for referral routing:** the platform does not tell individual stylists that a specific caller asked about them, to prevent multiple stylists from competing over/pestering the same lead. The salon owner instead sees an activity metric (e.g., "5 stylists were sent info today") without exposing who called whom — the system has no way of knowing, and doesn't need to know, whether the caller actually followed through.
10. **Known bug surfaced in the partner's own test of the demo:** asked to book an appointment on a Sunday when the salon is closed, the AI said "I will notify the salon" instead of recognizing the closure — it only surfaced the closure when separately asked "what are your hours on Sunday?" Flagged to the build team as needing the closed-day logic to proactively catch booking requests, not just answer when directly queried about hours.
11. **A2P/onboarding form specifics walked through live:** required fields include legal business name, EIN, business address, and authorized-representative contact info; business category must be picked from a **fixed drop-down list** for SMS/A2P purposes, and there is **no "personal services" category** — a hair salon was mapped to **Retail** as the closest fit (the rep used Claude to reason through the category options, noting "Google's not as smart" for this kind of judgment call). A missing terms-of-service page on the client's site was flagged as a real risk to approval; the partner kept a reusable, plug-and-play salon terms-of-service template ready to paste in exactly for this situation.
12. **A2P rejection flow praised as clear and actionable** by the partner from past experience: on rejection, the system explicitly names which fields are missing or wrong; you fix them and resubmit — contrasted favorably against a black-box rejection process.
13. **Commercial sequencing decision, explicit reasoning given:** launch with only the one-time setup fee first, not the monthly managed service, specifically so the client isn't locked into a year-long commitment before the AI receptionist is proven out — "if the salon ups and quits on them, they're still on the hook for it" under a locked annual plan. Add the monthly service later once the client is happy.
14. **Order/activation mechanics:** Conversations AI line item set to $0 for the first month on the order so the client can trial it live at zero cost before being switched onto the paid subscription; the phone number gets claimed as soon as the order is accepted/paid, even while A2P registration is still pending — so setup and testing of everything except live SMS can proceed during the A2P wait.
15. **Email deliverability fix applied live during the call:** outgoing platform emails default to a "businessmail.io" sending domain and can land in spam; connecting the partner's own owned email domain (Marketing → Email Settings → "Use my own email domain" → Add Domain → add the DNS records at the registrar) fixes this and was recommended as a setup step the partner had originally skipped.
16. **Fulfillment-form conventions for an AI-receptionist order specifically:** put the *partner's* own name/email/phone in the "first/last name" fields (not the end client's), select "AI receptionist" as the build type (chat receptionist would require website back-end credentials; voice receptionist does not), and attach the separate knowledge and workflow documents directly to the form so the build team has everything without hunting through email threads.

## Date unknown — Heather Gatlin, Megan, Alyssa Markham, Brian Heppner (partner ops team) & Andrew Claus, Logan Ochitwa (Vendasta/Kixie)

**Source:** [CALL] RFP detail review for a multi-agent call-center integration (Vendasta CRM + Kixie), primarily CRM/pipeline/telephony scope — filed here only for the AI-response-behavior portion

Most of this call is CRM/pipeline/dispositions/Kixie call-routing detail that belongs under the CRM topic, not ai-workforce (see flag at end of report). The one genuinely ai-workforce-relevant portion:

1. **AI conversational responses are instant/on-by-default across every channel** — SMS (including automatic "missed-call text-back" when an inbound call isn't answered), the AI voice agent answering calls, and AI monitoring of a shared email inbox if that channel is turned on.
2. **AI responses can be "snoozed" per contact** — an agent working a specific conversation directly can snooze the AI for a set duration (example given: "snooze the AI for at least an hour") so it stops auto-responding to that contact while the human handles it; once the snooze period ends, the AI automatically resumes monitoring that same thread. If the human resolves the issue but the customer reaches back out again later (example: 45 minutes after the snooze expires), the AI picks the conversation back up rather than leaving it unattended.
3. **AI responses can also be turned off entirely, indefinitely, for a specific contact** (e.g., a multi-day, multi-stakeholder issue where a human owns the whole thread) — with a manual **"Wake Up"** button to resume AI monitoring on that contact whenever the agent is ready to hand it back.

## Date unknown — a Vendasta solutions architect with an agency owner building a multi-client AI-employee automation practice (and his outsourced automation developers)

**Source:** [CALL] Architecture/automation troubleshooting session on chaining multiple AI Employees together for a lead-qualification-to-nurture funnel

Core platform constraint clarified directly by the architect: for customer/prospect-facing use (as opposed to internal/back-office AI Employees), **only one AI Employee can be assigned to a given communication channel at a time** — e.g., only one employee can own SMS conversations for the account. This directly conflicted with the partner's mental model of wanting distinctly-named AI "staff" (a chat receptionist, an inside-sales rep, a nurture specialist, an enrollment specialist, etc.) each independently replying over the same channel.

The actual technique used to fake a multi-persona experience: since it's really one underlying employee-per-channel handling the conversation, the "different staff members" the end customer perceives are created by **varying the sender name/signature** at each automation stage (e.g., a named "enrollment specialist" persona reaches out at one stage, a different named persona takes over after a tour is booked) — not by actually switching which AI Employee is live on the channel. Where genuine AI-driven persona-switching within a single employee is needed, the architect's guidance was to list out each persona (name + short description of when to use it) inside that one employee's role prompt, capped at roughly **2–3 personas max** to avoid degrading response quality ("more than three would be pushing it... let's not make it too complicated for the AI").

"Capability" is defined precisely as a Vendasta platform concept: a capability = a **prompt module plus, optionally, one or more tools**. A "tool" is a connection either to another product in the Vendasta ecosystem or to an external platform/webhook (e.g., n8n, Zapier). Native lead-capture (asking for name/phone/email and saving as a CRM contact) is an example of a built-in capability that ships with the chat receptionist by default.

Custom capability/tool authoring: partners can build entirely custom capabilities (e.g., calling an external webhook) by adding a tool, specifying only GET/POST support (no native OAuth 2 — static API key/token auth only), mapping headers/parameters, and marking each parameter's type, whether it's required, and whether its value is **set by the AI dynamically** or hard-coded/static. The tool must also be explicitly referenced by name inside the employee's prompt so the AI knows when/how to invoke it.

WhatsApp as an AI Employee channel: positioned as an external, customer-facing channel analogous to SMS (not an internal-orchestration channel), particularly relevant outside North America where WhatsApp is the dominant consumer messaging channel. Requires a WhatsApp Business account.

Longer-term "orchestration layer" vision acknowledged but unbuilt: the partner described a personally-built external "super agent" directing a team of other AI agents like a chief of staff. The architect confirmed this direction ("orchestration layer") is part of Vendasta's long-term roadmap but explicitly not something built yet, and declined to speculate on timeline: "that's not for me to decide, I just know what there is to know about the platform."
